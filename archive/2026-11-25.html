  • India's rail rolling stock and wagon makers sell freight wagons, metro coaches and components to Indian Railways, metro corporations and private wagon buyers.
  • Revenue depends on government tenders and capex cycles, while cost is driven by steel prices, imported components and fabrication capacity.
What the company does
  • Jupiter Wagons makes rail freight wagons, load bodies for commercial vehicles and metal fabricated components, with a large share of revenue from Indian Railways and wagon buyers.
  • It is one of India's largest private wagon makers by capacity, and the new Woojin joint venture moves it into metro coaches and propulsion systems.
Why it moved today
  • The stock hit a 20% upper circuit to ₹222.50 after Jupiter Wagons announced an 80:20 joint venture with South Korea's Woojin Industrial Systems to make metro coaches and propulsion systems in India.
  • The rally snapped an eight-session losing streak in which the stock had fallen 14%, and about 25 million shares traded on the NSE by 9:55 AM, worth ₹490.64 crore.
The numbers (Screener)
  • The stock trades at a P/E of 55.2 with a book value of ₹71.0, a market cap of ₹9,506 crore and a dividend yield of 0.45%.
  • September 2026 quarter sales were ₹772 crore with net profit of ₹46 crore and EPS of ₹1.12, after ₹671 crore sales and ₹26 crore profit in the June 2026 quarter.
What the numbers say
  • ROCE of 9.14% and ROE of 6.37% are modest for a capital-intensive fabricator, and the P/E of 55.2 looks expensive against that return on capital.
  • Sales fell from ₹890 crore in December 2025 to ₹772 crore in September 2026, so the 20% move reflects the joint venture news rather than an improving earnings trend.

Dabur India Ltd (DABUR, +7.77%)

What the sector does
  • India's FMCG sector sells packaged food, beverages, home and personal care products to households through distributors, modern trade and e-commerce.
  • Revenue is driven by volume growth and price hikes, while cost is set by crude derivatives, palm oil and ad spend; HUL, ITC, Nestle and Dabur are the main listed players.
What the company does
  • Dabur sells Ayurvedic and natural health care products, foods and home and personal care items, reaching 8 of every 10 Indian households through a wide distribution network.
  • It earns from brands such as Dabur, Vatika and Real, with 3 brands above ₹1,000 crore revenue and 16 brands in the ₹100-500 crore range.
Why it moved today
  • Dabur shares rose 7.77% to ₹430 on 25 November 2026 as the Nifty FMCG index gained 3.18%, with HUL and Godrej Consumer up 6% each, according to Business Standard.
  • The company gave no fresh filing that day, and the stock had already gained 12.72% in a week after touching a 52-week low of ₹354.30 on 27 October, Equitypandit reported.
The numbers (Screener)
  • Dabur trades at a P/E of 37.3 with a market cap of ₹76,011 crore, a book value of ₹64.8 per share and a dividend yield of 1.92%, per Screener data.
  • September 2026 quarter sales were ₹3,563 crore, up 12% year on year, with net profit of ₹499 crore and an operating margin of 18%.
What the numbers say
  • At a P/E of 37.3, Dabur trades at a premium to its own recent history but its ROCE of 20.3% and ROE of 17.0% show the business still earns well on capital.
  • September quarter sales growth of 12% was the strongest in the table, yet operating margin slipped to 18% from 21% in December 2025, so the rally rests on volume recovery rather than margin expansion.

Apl Apollo Tubes Ltd (APLAPOLLO, -11.62%)

What the sector does
  • Steel tubes and pipes makers sell MS black pipes, galvanised tubes, pre-galvanised tubes, structural ERW tubes and hollow sections to construction, housing, irrigation, solar and engineering buyers.
  • Revenue tracks steel prices and construction demand, while cost is dominated by hot-rolled coil, so spreads and capacity utilisation decide margins; key players include Tata Steel, Jindal Saw, Ratnamani Metals and APL Apollo.
What the company does
  • APL Apollo Tubes runs 10 plants making over 1,500 varieties of steel tubes and hollow sections from Delhi NCR, selling branded structural steel products to infrastructure, housing, irrigation and solar customers.
  • It earns from converting steel coil into branded tubes at scale, with ₹7,002 crore sales in the September 2026 quarter and a ₹41,083 crore market capitalisation.
Why it moved today
  • The stock fell 11.62% to ₹1,476 after Tata Steel agreed to buy the promoters' 26.38% stake at about ₹2,100 a share and launch an open offer for 26% more at ₹2,301.4, per [livemint.com](https://www.livemint.com/market/stock-market-news/why-apl-apollo-tubes-shares-crashed-15-today-to-plunge-at-52-week-low-downgrade-from-brokerages-after-tata-steel-deal-11795579639211.html).
  • Brokerages cut targets on the deal: Axis Capital to ₹1,420 from ₹2,400, ICICI Securities to ₹1,345 from ₹2,200, and Nuvama downgraded to Hold with ₹1,459 fair value, per [moneycontrol.com](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/apl-apollo-tubes-stock-brokerages-slash-price-targets-on-debt-earnings-concerns-following-tata-steel-deal-14088483.html).
The numbers (Screener)
  • The stock trades at a P/E of 31.4 and 7.10 times book value of ₹207, with ROCE of 31.8% and ROE of 25.4%, per Screener data on 25 November 2026.
  • September 2026 quarter sales rose 27% year on year to ₹7,002 crore and net profit rose to ₹382 crore, with EPS of ₹13.77, against ₹5,531 crore sales and ₹302 crore profit a year earlier.
What the numbers say
  • The P/E of 31.4 sits above the sector's typical mid-teens to low-twenties range for tube makers, and the 7.10 times book value leaves little room if the Tata Steel deal dilutes earnings as Axis Capital's 52% FY28 EPS cut implies.
  • ROCE of 31.8% and ROE of 25.4% show the core tube business earns well on capital, but the ₹28,000 crore debt-funded acquisition of Tata Steel's tubes and colour-coated businesses could lift net debt-to-EBITDA to around seven times, per Axis Capital, changing that picture.

Sun Tv Network Ltd (SUNTV, -9.21%)

What the sector does
  • Indian television broadcasting sells advertising slots to brands and carriage fees to cable and DTH distributors, with subscription revenue shared through the cable network.
  • Revenue rises with viewership and ad rates, while content, film and distribution costs are the main expenses; Zee Entertainment, Star India and Viacom18 are the big players.
What the company does
  • Sun TV Network runs Sun TV and a bouquet of Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Bengali and Marathi channels, plus FM radio stations and Sun Pictures film production.
  • It earns from advertising and subscription fees paid by viewers and distributors, and reported ₹940 crore sales in the September 2026 quarter.
Why it moved today
  • Sun TV shares fell 7.86% on November 25 as the Nifty Media index dropped 4.44% to 1,661.10, with nine of ten constituents declining, Moneycontrol reported.
  • The fall followed an 11.88% gain in the media index over the past month and a 21.77% rise over the past year, which the report attributed to profit booking.
The numbers (Screener)
  • The stock trades at a P/E of 15.3 with a market cap of ₹24,316 crore, a book value of ₹341 per share and a dividend yield of 2.03%.
  • September 2026 quarter sales were ₹940 crore with net profit of ₹365 crore and EPS of ₹9.24, against ₹1,458 crore sales and ₹619 crore profit in the June 2026 quarter.
What the numbers say
  • ROCE of 16.4% and ROE of 12.2% show the business earns reasonably on capital, though the five-year sales growth of 6.41% is slow.
  • The September quarter's ₹940 crore sales and ₹365 crore profit are well below the June quarter's ₹1,458 crore and ₹619 crore, so the 9.21% fall reflects profit booking after a strong run rather than a weak quarter.
