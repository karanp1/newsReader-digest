Every digital payment leaves a trace, and a trail of transactions can reveal a person's tastes, habits and priorities, not just amounts. 11 Oct 2026

India expects about ₹7.5 lakh crore of private investment and 30 GW of private nuclear capacity by 2047, Atomic Energy Commission chairman Ajit Kumar Mohanty told PTI. EconomyInvesting in sector and thematic funds

11 Oct 2026

Asset managers keep launching sector and thematic fund offers, and this piece asks whether they belong in your portfolio. Sector funds bet on one industry like banking or chemicals; thematic funds spread a bet across several, such as defense, which spans capital goods, automobiles and chemicals. Both come as active or passive funds, including index funds and ETFs. The author frames them as satellite holdings, the trading slice meant to beat the market, rather than core goal-based investments. A catch: these funds tend to launch when investor interest is high, not when a sector looks set to outperform. Mid-cap and small-cap active funds may be a better route, since managers rotate sectors for you.

Economy‘Excess’: More to pay, not more to receive

11 Oct 2026

An excess, also called a deductible, is the first slice of any claim you pay before your insurer pays anything. It is different from co-payment, which is a percentage of the bill rather than a flat sum. India's general insurance industry is reportedly considering a compulsory 10% co-payment on all new hospitalisation policies from the new year, in exchange for a lower premium. The logic is risk-sharing: small claims cost insurers more in paperwork than they pay out, so an excess pushes you to skip them. Voluntary excesses trade a premium discount for that risk. Read the numbers before agreeing.

Bitcoin at $82,965 as M2 chart predicts $250,000 to $300,000

11 Oct 2026

Bitcoin trades at $82,965 as of October 11, 2026, down 5.3% so far this year. A global M2 chart circulating in crypto discussions points to a much higher price, and Abra CEO Bill Barhydt cited it on The Pomp Podcast on October 8: "If you look at the charts that everybody likes to show with global M2 versus Bitcoin, they're all saying Bitcoin should be around 250 to 300k-ish in that range."

M2 measures how much money exists in an economy, including cash, checking accounts, and easily accessible savings like money market funds. The global version aggregates these figures across major economies. The chart adjusts the money supply line forward by a number of weeks chosen subjectively, on the belief that new money takes time to reach riskier assets. That lag makes the chart look accurate in hindsight while offering no genuine predictive power, according to the article.

Barhydt also cited a speaker's claim that Bitcoin must reach $300,000 within 24 months, the only concrete, testable prediction in the piece. For context, Bitcoin started 2026 at $87,498 and traded at $110,769 a year earlier. It needs a 12.1x rise to hit $1 million, which would put its market value at about $20.1 trillion from roughly $1.67 trillion today. The M2 chart is a talking point, not a forecast.

Analysts said currency moves, US rate expectations, geopolitical developments and investment demand will decide whether gold holds its recovery or stays range-bound. Record $31 billion into gold ETFs while futures traders sold

11 Oct 2026

Investors put a record $31 billion into global physically backed gold ETFs in the third quarter, according to the World Gold Council's September report. In September alone they added $10 billion, even as gold fell 8.5% and ended the month around $4,176 an ounce. Global holdings reached a record 4,256 metric tons. North American ETFs took in $12 billion in the quarter, with US funds adding $3.8 billion in September.

The buying ran against the usual script. Gold normally struggles when Treasury yields and the dollar rise, because higher yields raise the cost of holding an asset that pays no income. In September the 10-year Treasury yield rose 53 basis points and the dollar index gained about 2%. Investors kept buying anyway. The 10-year yield was 5.22% on Oct. 8, versus 3.97% on Feb. 27, and hit a period high of 5.31% on Oct. 5.

Short-term traders went the other way. Managed-money net long positions in Comex gold futures fell by about 84 metric tons during September. UBS strategist Giovanni Staunovo said ETF holdings have stayed unusually resilient with steady inflows, while non-commercial accounts cut net-long positions in futures and options. That split matters: selling through futures can outweigh physical investment flows over short periods, especially when yield changes prompt investors to cut speculative exposure.

HAL rolls out 2,000th aero-engine, MIDHANI supplies key alloys

11 Oct 2026

HAL's Koraput Division rolled out its 2,000th aero-engine on 6 October 2026, an AL-31FP that powers the Su-30MKI fighter fleet. The division has been building engines since 1964, so this marks six decades of propulsion manufacturing. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh, Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi and senior HAL leadership attended.

The milestone rests on domestic materials. Mishra Dhatu Nigam (MIDHANI) is delivering single-crystal superalloys and other high-temperature metals for several indigenous aero-engine programmes run by HAL. These alloys are built to survive the heat and stress inside a modern engine. MIDHANI Chairman and Managing Director Dr S.V.S. Narayana Murty said domestic development of these critical materials is fundamental to full self-reliance in aero-engine production.

The point for HAL is supply chain control. Local alloy capacity reduces dependence on foreign suppliers for strategic aerospace materials, which matters when engine programmes scale. The 2,000th engine is a volume proof point; the alloy work is what keeps that volume running.

Tejas Mk1A waits on software as 29 airframes sit built

11 Oct 2026

HAL has manufactured 29 Tejas Mk1A jets with all hardware modifications complete, but deliveries to the Indian Air Force are held up by software. Integration and validation of the AESA radar software from Israel's ELTA, the electronic warfare suite and weapon launcher software are still under testing, according to HAL Chairman and Managing Director Ravi K.

The Mk1A is the upgraded variant of the indigenous Light Combat Aircraft, powered by the F404 engine. HAL is working with the IAF, the Aeronautical Development Agency and quality assurance regulators to finish certification and start deliveries.

For HAL, this is the near-term execution test. The airframes exist; the certification does not. Until the digital systems are signed off, revenue and squadron availability stay on the tarmac.

Tejas Mk2 first flight targeted for Q3 2027

11 Oct 2026

HAL expects the Tejas Mk2 prototype to make its first flight in the third quarter of 2027, with engine ground runs planned by March 2027. The Mk2 is a heavier 4.5-generation multirole fighter meant to replace the IAF's Mirage 2000, Jaguar and MiG-29 fleets. It carries a more powerful GE F414 engine, an internal radar jammer, infrared search and track (IRST) systems and network-centric warfare tools.

HAL is the principal manufacturing partner to the Aeronautical Development Agency on the programme. The AMCA, India's fifth-generation stealth fighter, stays under ADA direction, and HAL gave no specific milestones for it.

HAL also wants a civilian business. It is negotiating licensed production of Russia's SJ-100 regional aircraft with United Aircraft Corporation, following an October 2025 memorandum of understanding, with a goal of assembling around 200 aircraft over the next decade. Its own civil line includes the Dhruv NG helicopter, the Civil LUH and the Hindustan-228, with four Hindustan-228s operating in Guyana. HAL is also building a commercial MRO hub in Nashik with Airbus for A320 C-checks. The timelines are the thing to watch.

The lightest model, GPT-6 Luna, now handles code implementation with clear requirements. GPT-6.1 Sol covers standard design, data analysis, and document prep. GPT-6 Astra, the most capable, is reserved for advanced judgment like architecture design. Teams start light and move up as complexity grows. Fast mode is off by default and available on request. Budget caps sit at department, group, and individual levels, with mature businesses asked for roughly 20% more cost efficiency and new businesses given generous budgets on purpose. Teams kept development speed by running tasks in parallel.

The part worth watching is what LegalOn does next. Having solved cost, it admits it still cannot tell whether faster development produces customer value, and it is building a metric that ties the value of each feature release to the AI costs spent on it. That is the right question, and most companies adopting AI are not asking it yet.

OpenAIDisrupting AI-enabled “false front” operations

08 Oct 2026

OpenAI banned two covert influence operations, one from Russia and one from Iran, that used its models to build "false front" entities, fake organizations with personas like journalists or think tanks that disguise who is really behind the messaging. The Iranian operation ran seven fake "journalist" personas pitching long-form articles to small and mid-sized outlets worldwide. The Russian one co-opted unwitting people in Latin America to run a supposed think tank.

The Russia-origin operation scored Category 5 on the IO Breakout Scale, a 1-to-6 rating of an influence campaign's reach, the first Category 5 operation OpenAI has disrupted since it started reporting. Iran's reached Category 4. Both got content into mainstream outlets rather than just social media, which matches a pattern across the 30 covert operations OpenAI has exposed in two and a half years: campaigns that land in real publications reach further than those stuck on fake social accounts.

What stands out is how familiar the playbook is. The Iranian personas echo "Alice Donovan," a fake journalist fronting for Russian military intelligence whose work ran in Western outlets in 2016-17; the Russian think tank echoes PeaceData, a fake news outlet staffed by unwitting writers in 2020. AI mainly made the workflows easier, adding scale, fluency and editorial polish.

My view: disclosure is the real weapon here, since both earlier fronts folded once exposed, and that is the part worth watching as these operations get cheaper to run.

AnthropicIntroducing the Anthropic Cyber Mission

08 Oct 2026

Anthropic is launching the Anthropic Cyber Mission, a long-term program to help defenders secure software and critical systems. It starts with two efforts: the Critical Infrastructure Defense Program, which puts Claude models, on-site engineers, and threat research behind the companies that protect power grids, water systems, and transportation networks, and OSS Scanner, which gives open-source projects free, regular security scans from Anthropic's strongest models. Founding partners in the infrastructure program include Accenture, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC, and Rockwell Automation.

The reason is straightforward. Frontier models can be misused to find and exploit vulnerabilities, and state-sponsored adversaries have spent years gaining footholds in exposed systems. The defenders who know this equipment best have faced severe resource shortages. Critical infrastructure runs on operational technology, the controllers and industrial networks built to last decades, which often cannot be taken offline to patch, so known flaws stay open for years. Anthropic says it merged its earlier Project Glasswing into an expanded Cyber Verification Program after finding that locating vulnerabilities is now easier than verifying, prioritizing, and fixing them.

The bet is that AI helps most where human capacity is thinnest. Anthropic is starting with a small cohort of providers to learn what actually works, and says it will expand the mission into new areas. Watch whether the fixes get verified and shipped, not just found.
