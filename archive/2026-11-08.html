Reliance

  • Reliance Consumer Products expanded its Independence biscuit portfolio to nine variants and 28 SKUs, starting at ₹5, rolling out first in Tamil Nadu.
  • India's biscuit market was ₹55,100 crore in FY24 and is projected to reach ₹90,300 crore by FY29, growing around 10.4% annually.
  • Britannia and Parle together hold more than half the market, with Britannia at around 32% and Parle at 29% in FY25 estimates.
  • Britannia CEO Rakshit Hargave said disrupting the ₹5 and ₹10 biscuit price points is harder than in cola, where a ₹5 discount can unsettle rivals.
  • General trade accounted for about 71% of branded biscuit sales in FY24, with modern trade at 21% and e-commerce at 8%.

Sources: m.economictimes.com

Outlook: Neutral

Reliance's scale and distribution could pressure incumbents, but Britannia and Parle control over half the market and Rs 5 packs leave little room to undercut. The split is distribution reach versus entrenched pricing.

Flipkart

  • Myntra and Flipkart's combined share of India's online fashion orders fell to 76.8% in the 2026 festive season from 89% a year earlier, per Fynd's Festive Season Report 2026.
  • Average discounts in online fashion rose to 46% from 34% in 2025, with nearly half of all orders placed at discounts above 50%.
  • Footwear became the largest online fashion category at 52.7% of orders and 58.4% of sales value, while clothing's order share fell to 34.5% from 48%.
  • Tier 2 cities raised their share of festive fashion orders to about 35% from 19% in 2025, and Tier 1 cities took 43% of orders but 56% of GMV.
  • Flipkart listed the Motorola Moto G37 Power at ₹14,999, a 43% discount on its ₹26,499 MRP, and the Motorola Edge 70 Pro at ₹35,149 with an Axis Bank offer.

Sources: businessworld.in, gizbot.com, timesbull.com

Outlook: Bearish

Myntra and Flipkart's combined fashion order share dropped 12.2 percentage points to 76.8% as Ajio, Amazon and Jiomart gained, while discounts deepened to 46% from 34%. That points to heavier promotional spending to defend a shrinking share.

Godigit Insurance

  • Go Digit reported Q2 FY27 net profit of ₹142 crore, down 31% from ₹207 crore a year earlier, as claims and flood losses hit earnings.
  • The claims ratio climbed to 62.4% from 48.2% a year earlier, and the combined insurance service ratio rose to 99.8% from 94.9%.
  • Gross written premium fell 3.6% to ₹2,571 crore after the insurer exited health reinsurance inward and government health business on unattractive pricing.
  • Excluding flood losses, the combined ratio was 97.4% and profit after tax excluding mark-to-market impact would have been ₹255 crore.
  • Solvency ratio stood at 2.40x against the regulatory minimum of 1.50x, and assets under management rose 15.9% to ₹24,371 crore.

Sources: bfsi.eletsonline.com, marketsmojo.com

Outlook: Bearish

Claims ratio jumped to 62.4% and combined ratio to 99.8%, cutting profit 31% despite flat ex-exit premium. Flood losses and soft motor pricing keep underwriting strained; only solvency at 2.40x cushions.

Gold

  • Goldman Sachs cut its end-2026 gold target to $4,150 per ounce from $4,650, and end-2027 to $4,800 from $5,400, citing a hawkish Fed.
  • China's central bank bought about 25.8 metric tons of gold in October, a 24th straight month, as prices fell almost 7% in October below $4,000 an ounce.
  • Spot gold has fallen about 20% from its February peak above $5,000 an ounce to around $4,000, its cheapest valuation since 2022, per LS Securities.
  • Gold Fields reported Q3 attributable production of 605,000 ounces, expects 2026 output at the upper end of guidance, and holds $981 million in net debt.
  • Gold miners rallied, with Newmont, Agnico Eagle, Barrick and Franco-Nevada up 10% to 16% this week, as spot gold posted its best week since August.

Sources: finance.yahoo.com, finance.biggo.com, finance.yahoo.com, finance.yahoo.com

Outlook: Neutral

Goldman's lower targets and Fed hike expectations weigh on prices, but China's 24th month of buying and record Q3 central bank purchases of 321 tons support the floor. Official demand versus tighter policy splits the outlook.
