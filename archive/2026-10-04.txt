IT sector sees an uptick in fresher hiring after two years of slowdown

India’s $300 billion technology and

IT services industry is seeing a revival in fresher onboarding after nearly two years of subdued hiring.

Even so, entry-level recruitment remains below mid-level hiring as demand for experienced AI talent stays strong.

Number wise: Fresher hiring, which fell to a record low of around 110,000 in FY26, is expected to rise to 115,000-135,000 in the current fiscal year, according to recruitment firm Quess Group. That implies a 4.5-22.7% increase.

The decline had been steady:

FY22: 380,000

FY23: 250,000

FY24: 155,000

FY25: 125,000

FY26: ~110,000

A rebound began in the June quarter, with TCS, Infosys and HCLTech together recruiting about 19,000 entry-level staff, Nasscom said in its September quarter report covering 29 IT services firms.

Higher bar for freshers: Experts told us the bar for entry-level talent has risen. Firms increasingly want specialised capabilities in AI, cloud, data and cybersecurity to improve revenue visibility, meet client demands and ensure deployment readiness with limited ramp-up time.

- Bitcoin Investors Who Bought Last Year's Rally Are Selling As Price Nears Their Break-Even, Glassnode Says
  03 Oct 2026 | https://www.tradingview.com/news/stocktwits:517fdf221094b:0-bitcoin-investors-who-bought-last-year-s-rally-are-selling-as-price-nears-their-break-even-glassnode-says/
  Bitcoin buyers from last year's rally are selling at the fastest daily pace of 2025, according to on-chain analytics firm Glassnode, because the price has fallen back to what they paid. Glassnode grouped holders by purchase date and calculated each group's average cost basis, the price at which they break even. Buyers from six to 12 months ago paid about $89,000 on average, and buyers from one to two years ago paid about $97,000. With Bitcoin trading near $84,673, both groups are underwater. The six-to-12-month cohort needs roughly a 5% gain to break even, the older group nearly 15%. ¶ That matters because underwater holders often wait for the price to return to their cost basis, then sell to exit without a loss. Each cost basis becomes a level where selling pressure builds, which helps explain why Bitcoin stalled below $85,000. Glassnode found the selling is concentrated in these 2025 buyers, while holders who bought during the decline are not moving coins. Analyst Ted Pillows says a weekly close above $87,500 is needed to confirm a rally; otherwise he sees a decent chance of a drop below $80,000. Watch that weekly close. If it fails, the cost-basis wall above will keep capping any bounce.
