- Bitcoin order books deepen a year after $19 billion crash
  10 Oct 2026 | https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/10/one-year-after-10-10-bitcoin-and-ether-liquidity-have-rebuilt-but-other-altcoins-still-face-risks
  Bitcoin's order book is deeper now than on any date measured since the start of 2025. About $11.7 million sits within 1% of the price, roughly 75% more than on crash day a year earlier and up from about $9 million at the start of this year. Depth is the value of resting buy and sell orders near the current price, so a deeper book absorbs larger trades with less price movement. Bitcoin trades about one-third cheaper than before the crash, so the improvement reflects more capital from market makers, not just lower coin values. Ether's recovery is stronger in tight bands, with depth within 0.5% of price more than doubling to about $4.2 million. ¶ Altcoins went the other way. Dollar depth in a basket of altcoins is down about a third since the start of 2025 at 5% from price, to around $2 million. Spot trading has not recovered either. Weekly spot volume on centralized exchanges averaged about $279 billion over the four weeks to Sept. 27, nearly two-thirds below the $801 billion traded in the week of the crash. Activity bottomed in August at around $135 billion and has doubled since, but remains well short of crash levels. ¶ The rebuilt books got an early test this week. As the market sold off, bitcoin's 1% depth fell about 12% between Oct. 7 and Oct. 8. For holders, the split matters: majors have deeper liquidity to absorb selling, while altcoins and spot activity still lag.

  10 Oct 2026 | https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/10/bitcoin-s-usd19-billion-wake-up-call-one-year-later-has-crypto-learned-anything

Exchanges have strong financial incentives to keep offering leveraged products. Connors warned another October 10th is still possible because levered products have not gone away. Open interest tracks outstanding derivatives contracts, while funding rates reflect the cost of holding perpetual futures positions.
