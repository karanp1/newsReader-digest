Amber Enterprises India Ltd (AMBER, +9.07%)

What the sector does
  • India's room air conditioner (RAC) industry sells split and window units to households and businesses through dealers, with demand peaking in summer and fading in the monsoon.
  • Revenue depends on summer weather, real estate, power supply and replacement demand; costs are copper, aluminium, compressors and currency, and the biggest players are LG, Voltas, Blue Star and Daikin.
What the company does
  • Amber Enterprises is an original design and equipment manufacturer (ODM) that builds room air conditioners and components for brands, holding a 23.6% share of India's RAC market.
  • It also makes electronics, railway sub-systems and consumer durables, earning from manufacturing contracts; with ₹23,883 crore market cap it is smaller than LG and Voltas but larger than Blue Star.
Why it moved today
  • The stock rose 9.07% after Q2 FY27 revenue jumped 60% year-on-year to ₹2,627 crore, beating the 19% growth estimate, with the electronics division up 94% against a 30% forecast, Moneycontrol reported.
  • Context, not the cause: the same quarter showed a consolidated net loss of ₹66 crore versus a ₹32 crore loss a year earlier, operating margin of 3.7% and interest cost of ₹95 crore.
The numbers (Screener)
  • Stock P/E is 142 and price to book 5.47 times, with ROCE 10.3% and ROE 5.99%, against a book value of ₹1,238 per share.
  • September 2026 quarter sales were ₹2,627 crore, up 59.51% year-on-year, but operating profit fell to ₹98 crore (3.7% margin) and net profit was a loss of ₹66 crore.
What the numbers say
  • At a P/E of 142, Amber trades far above the peer median of 40.16 and above LG at 61.56, Voltas at 77.26 and Blue Star at 60.89, even though its ROCE of 10.3% is below the peer median of 10.93%.
  • Sales grew 59.51% year-on-year, the fastest among the named peers, but the September quarter swung to a ₹66 crore loss from ₹3 crore profit in June 2026, so the 9.07% move looks like a reaction to revenue growth rather than earnings.
