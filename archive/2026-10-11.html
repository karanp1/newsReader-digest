Bitcoin holds near $83,000 a year after $19 billion crash

11 Oct 2026

Bitcoin traded around $83,062 on Sunday, roughly 34% below its October 6, 2025 record of $126,080. It fell to $80,308 during the week before recovering. A year ago, on October 10, 2025, it plunged from about $122,000 to $105,000 after President Donald Trump announced sweeping tariffs on Chinese imports, triggering more than $19 billion in liquidations across about 1.6 million accounts.

The conditions behind that crash remain. Perpetual futures, which let traders bet on price without owning bitcoin, are still a major part of trading, and exchanges keep offering leveraged products. Mark Connors of Risk Dimensions said positioning still drives short-term price, and roughly $3.3 billion in bitcoin short positions could be liquidated if price climbs toward $85,000. US spot bitcoin ETFs recorded more than $700 million in outflows this week.

For a holder, the anniversary is a reminder that derivatives, not on-chain demand, set the near-term price. Connors said the four-year halving cycle has changed and carries less signal than before. The market bent but did not break.

Bitcoin and ether order books deepen, altcoins and spot lag

10 Oct 2026

Bitcoin's order book is deeper now than on crash day, at the start of this year, or at the start of 2025. About $11.7 million sat within 1% of the price on October 7, roughly 75% more than a year earlier and up from about $9 million in January. Depth is the value of resting buy and sell orders near the current price. Bitcoin is about one-third cheaper than before the crash, so the deeper book reflects more capital from market makers, not just less valuable coins.

Ether's recovery is stronger in the tightest band. Depth within 0.5% of the price has more than doubled since crash day to about $4.2 million, and at 1% it is roughly $5.3 million. Altcoins went the other way: depth at 5% from the price is down about a third since the start of 2025, to around $2 million.

Spot trading has not recovered. Weekly spot volume averaged about $279 billion over the four weeks to September 27, nearly two-thirds below the $801 billion in the crash week. Market makers returned to the majors, but trading activity did not follow.

Individuals hold two-thirds of bitcoin, Satoshi about 4.6%

11 Oct 2026

Individuals hold about 14 million BTC, roughly two-thirds of the 21 million that will ever exist, according to River's 2026 adoption report. The largest single holder is probably bitcoin's pseudonymous creator, Satoshi Nakamoto, whose untouched stash is estimated at 968,000 coins, or 4.6% of the total. A Cointelegraph investigation in September 2026 put estimates between just under 900,000 and about 1.2 million BTC. Another 1.6 million coins are estimated permanently lost.

Among companies, Strategy holds the most at 848,000 BTC, about 4% of the total, while every other company combined holds about 3.9%. Neither Satoshi's coins nor the lost ones are likely to re-enter circulation, which shrinks the pool available to buyers.

For a holder, the supply picture is tight at the top and widely held at the base. That limits how much new corporate buying can move the float, and it means retail remains the dominant owner.

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh, Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi and senior HAL leadership attended.

The milestone rests on domestic materials. Mishra Dhatu Nigam (MIDHANI) is delivering single-crystal superalloys and other high-temperature metals for several indigenous aero-engine programmes run by HAL. These alloys are built to survive the heat and stress inside a modern engine. MIDHANI Chairman and Managing Director Dr S.V.S. Narayana Murty said domestic development of these critical materials is fundamental to full self-reliance in aero-engine production.

The point for HAL is supply chain control. Local alloy capacity reduces dependence on foreign suppliers for strategic aerospace materials, which matters when engine programmes scale. The 2,000th engine is a volume proof point; the alloy work is what keeps that volume running.

For HAL, this is the near-term execution test. The airframes exist; the certification does not. Until the digital systems are signed off, revenue and squadron availability stay on the tarmac.
