Analysis: Why NASA wants to build a nuclear reactor on the moon

07 Oct 2026

NASA plans a 20 kW nuclear reactor near the moon's south pole by 2030, enough for a few households on Earth. Russia and China's ILRS reactor would produce about 10 kW. The south pole draws attention because craters there hold water ice, which can be electrolysed into hydrogen and oxygen for rocket propellant. NASA scientists estimated 10 tonnes of oxygen would need roughly 68 kW, so 20 kW is a stepping stone toward larger reactor arrays. The Outer Space Treaty bars territorial claims, but Artemis Accords safety zones could let a country cluster reactors and limit rivals' access. Watch the technical hurdles: shielding, waste-heat radiators, and moondust.

ICE brings futures trading to London’s $190 billion-a-day physical gold market

06 Oct 2026

ICE, the exchange group that owns the New York Stock Exchange, launched precious metals futures trading in London this week, with contracts tied directly to the city's daily gold price auctions. Silver, platinum and palladium futures launched alongside. London is the world's dominant physical gold market, handling roughly $190 billion in daily over-the-counter trades, with about $1.4 trillion in bullion sitting in its vaults. Futures are standardized contracts to buy or sell metal at a set price later, and until now traders wanting that kind of exchange-traded exposure had to go to New York.

The gap was odd: London dominates physical trading but has lacked a major futures market for years. Two earlier attempts died, the London Gold Futures Market in 1985 and the London Metal Exchange's contract in 2022, both for lack of trading volume. ICE's edge is that it already runs the daily auctions that set benchmark prices, so it can link contracts to a benchmark it controls.

The bet is that a futures market can only survive where the pricing power already sits, and this time it does. Watch whether trading volume builds in the first year, since that is exactly what killed the last two tries.

Hindustan Aeronautics (HAL) marked two engine milestones at its Koraput division in Odisha on Tuesday. The new line will be able to build up to 100 Shakti engines a year once fully established by 2028-29, according to the government. Singh also said HAL will lift annual production of the AL-31FP engine, which powers the Indian Air Force's Su-30MKI fighters, from 30 to 50 units at an investment of ₹335 crore.

The stock rose on the news. HAL shares were up 3.5% at ₹4,833.45 on the BSE in intraday trade, against a 0.72% gain in the BSE Sensex, with combined volume on the NSE and BSE of 1.08 million shares, more than four times the average, according to Business Standard. The stock hit a 52-week high of ₹5,149.90 on 17 August 2026. Moneycontrol reported the shares up 2.01% at ₹4,774 at 11:32 am on the NSE.

The Shakti engine, co-developed with France's Safran, powers the Dhruv Advanced Light Helicopter, its armed Rudra variant and the Prachand Light Combat Helicopter. Each Prachand carries two Shakti engines. The armed forces plan to induct 156 Prachands, 66 for the Air Force and 90 for the Army, ordered from HAL in March 2025 under contracts worth ₹62,700 crore. HAL's existing helicopter order backlog also includes 25 Dhruv ALH, 12 Light Utility Helicopters and 10 Dhruv NG helicopters ordered by Pawan Hans, according to ICICI Securities. A further 110 LUH and 95 LCH helicopters have been planned but contracts are yet to be placed.

The AL-31FP expansion follows a September 2024 Ministry of Defence contract worth over ₹26,000 crore for 240 engines for the Su-30MKI fleet. HAL has delivered 63 engines so far, against a target of 60 by the end of the second year, the government said. The Koraput division has also overhauled more than 9,000 engines over its history.

Separately, HAL's CMD Ravi Kota said after completing the Tejas Mk1 trainer aircraft contract on 18 September 2026 that he is confident of delivering Tejas Mk1A aircraft this year. The company has received 10 engines for the aircraft from GE, according to Jefferies. Tejas Mk1A accounts for 43% of HAL's order book. Jefferies said the delivery of 10 aircraft this year would compare favourably with its assumption of 5, and that Indian Air Force acceptance of even one aircraft would give confidence in HAL's execution. The brokerage has a Buy rating on HAL with a target price of ₹6,800 per share.

On the wider propulsion front, Singh referred to a proposed investment of around ₹5,000 crore in an indigenous high-thrust engine programme, including India's first high-thrust engine testing facility and indigenisation of critical raw materials with Mishra Dhatu Nigam. DRDO's Gas Turbine Research Establishment has proposed co-developing a 120kN-class engine with Safran for the Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA), a proposal worth nearly ₹60,000 crore that awaits clearance from the Cabinet Committee on Security. The DRDO-developed Kaveri engine did not meet the thrust requirement for the Tejas, leaving that fighter dependent on GE's F404 engine, and delays in F404 deliveries have held up Tejas Mk1A production.

In a related development, Bengaluru-based Hical Technologies inaugurated a 60,000-square-foot facility in Devanahalli to make electromagnetic, electromechanical, hydraulic and control systems for commercial aircraft and defence programmes. Hical supplies components for Dassault Aviation's Rafale and is developing components for HAL's Tejas Mk2 programme. It is also building a 160,000-square-foot aerospace-qualified facility in Hosur, where groundbreaking was completed in July 2026.

What is not yet known: the timing of the first Shakti engine from the new line before the 2028-29 full-capacity target, the delivery schedule for the 240 AL-31FP engines beyond the 63 already supplied, and whether the AMCA engine proposal with Safran will receive CCS approval. The contracts for the additional 110 LUH and 95 LCH helicopters remain unplaced, so the revenue they represent is not yet in the order book.

The main risks are execution and supply chain. HAL's stock de-rated after Tejas Mk1A delays since 2024, and the F404 engine shortage is outside HAL's control. The Shakti line depends on Safran collaboration and imported components. The AL-31FP ramp-up faces the same global supply-chain and raw-material pressures the government acknowledged. The AMCA engine programme is a long-term effort with no guarantee of success, as the Kaveri experience shows.

My view: the engine announcements are a genuine capacity expansion that supports HAL's helicopter order book and reduces a bottleneck in delivering aircraft already contracted. The Tejas Mk1A delivery record this year will matter more to the investment case than the engine milestones, because that programme is 43% of the order book and the main reason the stock has lagged.

What to watch next: whether HAL delivers the 10 Tejas Mk1A aircraft it now expects this year, the pace of AL-31FP deliveries against the 240-engine order, any progress on the AMCA engine proposal at the CCS, and whether the pending LUH and LCH contracts are placed.

06 Oct 2026

Jio Platforms, the digital and telecom holding company of Reliance Industries, plans to open its initial public offering for subscription on October 21 and list its shares on October 28, according to sources cited by Reuters and other outlets. The company is seeking about $3.8 billion, which would make it India's largest-ever IPO, surpassing Hyundai Motor India's $2.9 billion offering in 2024. The National Stock Exchange of India raised $2.3 billion in its listing last month.

The issue consists entirely of fresh shares, with no offer-for-sale, meaning existing investors are not selling down. Jio plans to issue up to 27 crore new equity shares, about 2.9 per cent of its post-issue equity base. The anchor round, where large institutions get allotments before the public opens, is tentatively set for October 19. Subscription runs October 21 to 23. The final prospectus is expected to be filed with the Securities and Exchange Board of India in the week starting October 12, after the regulator gave its nod on August 28. Jio filed its draft papers in June.

Valuation estimates vary across sources. PTI, citing banking sources, reports an enterprise valuation of $143-146 billion, or at least Rs 12 lakh crore. Bloomberg, cited by CNBC TV18 and in a separate report, says Jio is seeking around Rs 11 lakh crore, or $114 billion. That is below earlier expectations of $130-170 billion. At $114 billion, Jio would be India's third most valuable listed company, behind Reliance Industries at about $169 billion and Bharti Airtel at about $116 billion. At the higher figure, it would rank among the top three by market capitalisation. The final pricing has not been determined and a decision is expected in the coming week.

About Rs 27,500 crore of the proceeds is earmarked to repay or prepay borrowings of Reliance Jio Infocomm, the telecom subsidiary. The rest goes to general corporate purposes. Reliance Industries owns 66.4 per cent of Jio Platforms. Meta and Google together hold about 17.7 per cent, from investments of Rs 43,574 crore and Rs 33,737 crore respectively in 2020. Neither has expressed interest in diluting, which is why the issue has no offer-for-sale component.

Jio Platforms operates mobile networks, artificial intelligence, cloud services and enterprise networks. As of June 2026 it had 26.85 crore 5G customers and about 1.5 crore fixed wireless access subscribers, which is home broadband delivered over mobile spectrum rather than a cable. For the 2025-26 financial year it reported profit after tax of Rs 30,053 crore, up 15 per cent, on revenue of Rs 1,46,885 crore. Those figures come from the draft prospectus.

The IPO lands in a busy Indian primary market. Funds raised between April and September set a record for the six-month period, with listing proceeds above $9 billion in the July-September quarter alone and total fundraising this year past $13 billion, according to Bloomberg data. Nearly 250 companies are in the pipeline to raise a combined Rs 4.65 trillion, about $48 billion, according to PRIME Database, including Carlsberg's India business and Prism, the parent of hotel aggregator Oyo. The broader stock market has fallen, and the National Stock Exchange had to cut the size of its own offering.

Jefferies raised Reliance's weight in its model portfolio to 7.5 per cent, arguing the stock trades at 8.4 times one-year forward EV/EBITDA, 23 per cent below its 10-year average. Several things remain unconfirmed. Reliance Industries did not respond to Reuters' request for comment, and Jio did not answer queries from other outlets. All dates, the valuation and the pricing are subject to change. The gap between the $114 billion and $143-146 billion figures is unresolved, and the sources in both cases spoke anonymously. The final papers with SEBI have not been filed.

The main risks sit around pricing and demand. A falling market has already forced one large issuer to shrink its offer, and Jio's own valuation expectations have come down from earlier ranges. A holding-company discount could apply to Reliance's 66.4 per cent stake after listing, though Motilal Oswal says the market already prices in 18-36 per cent. My view: the listing is material mainly because it puts a public price on Jio for the first time, which affects how the market values Reliance's stake, and because the debt repayment strengthens the telecom balance sheet. The operating business itself does not change on listing day.
