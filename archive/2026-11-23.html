Paper tiger: On the Green India Mission

22 Nov 2026

https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/paper-tiger/article71731271.ece

  • The Public Accounts Committee on November 19 criticised the Environment Ministry for the Green India Mission's virtual failure and for not issuing implementation guidelines for its revised form.
  • The original 10-year vision sought to expand forest and tree cover by 5 million hectares each, improve livelihoods for 3 million households, and sequester up to 60 million tonnes of carbon dioxide a year, with an outlay of ₹46,000 crore.
  • In 2014 the Cabinet approved ₹13,000 crore for 1.4 mha each of the first two objectives by 2018, but 81.5% was to come from CAMPA, NAP, and MGNREGS funds that never materialised.
  • Programmes to improve forest quality and forest cover fell short by 91.8% and 97.5% respectively against the 2014 target, as the Ministry built a large programme around resources it could not control.
  • The PAC's demand for an implementation timetable is insufficient; the Union government must dedicate adequate resources and formalise responsibility-sharing so climate action is timely.

Oyo eyes January-March IPO as investors peg valuation at around $2 billion

Ritesh Agarwal, CEO, Oyo

Oravel Stays, the parent company of hospitality firm Oyo, is

likely to launch its initial public offering (IPO) in the January-March quarter, with its valuation being pegged at Rs 20,000-22,000 crore ($2.1-2.3 billion), people briefed on the matter said.

The IPO plan: Oyo is expected to file a revised draft prospectus in the coming weeks, said one of the persons. The hospitality firm's IPO is expected to be entirely a primary capital raise, with none of the shareholders selling stakes through an offer-for-sale (OFS) component.

In an October filing, Oravel had cut its proposed IPO size by nearly half to Rs 3,350 crore, amid a recalibration of its fundraising plans.

Oyo’s response: Responding to ET's queries, a company spokesperson said, "The public issue timeline and valuation mentioned by you are inaccurate. We will share when we have any update."

A history of delays:

September 2021: Filed for an Rs 8,430-crore ($1.2 billion) IPO.

December 2022: Sebi returned the draft prospectus, asking Oyo to refile it with updated financials.

March 2023: Made a confidential filing for a smaller $400-600 million IPO.

May 2024: Withdrew the filing and opted for a private funding round.

December 2025: Made a fresh confidential filing for its third attempt, targeting a valuation of $5-7 billion.

June 2026: Filed an updated draft prospectus after receiving Sebi clearance.

Global footprint: More than 84% of its operating revenue now comes from outside India, with the US contributing 27% and Europe around 24%.

Dealership expansion becomes key to EV makers’ growth ambitions

Electric two-wheeler makers are

ramping up dealership networks to drive sales, reach newer markets and strengthen after-sales service as competition intensifies.

New players expand: Ola Electric is targeting more than 700 dealerships by March 2027.

Oben Electric is planning 200 additional showrooms by the end of this fiscal.

River Mobility is adding 10-15 outlets a month.

Ultraviolette aims to expand to 100 stores by March.

Ather Energy plans to resume network growth as supply improves.

Why it matters: Sanjay Tripathi, an automotive advisor who has worked with Hero MotoCorp and Yamaha, said India is a dealer-driven market. “Despite the product's superiority, distribution will make a difference,” he said.

Legacy players: Bajaj's Chetak has over 550 exclusive stores and is targeting 1,000 in about a year, while TVS sells its iQube through more than 2,800 dealerships.

Rising sales: With electric two-wheeler registrations rising 48% year-on-year to a record 2.24 lakh in October, distribution, service reach and dealer viability could become critical differentiators in the next phase of growth.
