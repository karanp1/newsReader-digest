OpenAI plans to dump hundreds of AI-generated solutions to unsolved math problems onto GitHub on Tuesday, according to people familiar with the plans who spoke to WIRED. OpenAI denies the characterization. Mathematicians describe the field as a showcase for OpenAI and Anthropic as both approach public offerings, with normal scientific process set aside. My view: the tools are real, but the labs are burning the goodwill of the people best placed to verify them, and that will cost more than any single release.

Global pick: South Korea’s AI chip giants are fighting for a bigger role in America’s AI boom

Rest of World

https://restofworld.org/2026/samsung-sk-hynix-ai-memory-chip-war-nvidia-openai/

Samsung has more than doubled its share of the high-bandwidth memory market in the past year, closing on SK Hynix, which had long led. The two South Korean firms together control 83% of global memory chip production. High-bandwidth memory is the specialized memory that lets AI processors pull huge amounts of data fast, so it sits inside nearly every system used to train and run AI models.

SK Hynix got its lead through more advanced core technology and an early supply relationship with Nvidia. Samsung has spent the past year signing its own deals to catch up: work with Nvidia on AI-RAN (embedding AI in cellular base stations) in March 2025, joining OpenAI's Stargate infrastructure project that October, an AI factory with Nvidia, and ChatGPT Enterprise rolled out to its employees. It is also working with OpenAI on next-generation chips. SK Hynix is building a $4 billion advanced packaging facility in Indiana.

For Nvidia and OpenAI, the contest matters because who supplies this component affects how fast and how cheaply they can build the next wave of AI infrastructure. Analysts say Nvidia's allocation decisions still determine market share more than anything else, and Samsung's real test is whether it can take supply share for Nvidia's Vera Rubin. The complication is China, roughly a third of the global market, where both firms have legacy plants and now face annual U.S. licensing plus rising domestic Chinese rivals. My view: watch Samsung's Vera Rubin allocation, since that single decision will show whether this is a real shift in leadership or just a stronger second place.
