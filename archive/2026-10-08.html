Bitcoin cost bases point to resistance at $88,000 and support near $82,300

07 Oct 2026

Bitcoin is trading below $84,000, and the buyers who bought in 2025 are the only yearly group still losing money. Their average purchase price sits near $88,000, so many are waiting to sell as soon as the price gets back to what they paid. That selling is likely to cap any rally around that level.

The idea of a "cost basis" is simple: it is the average price a group of investors paid. When price approaches that number, people who are underwater tend to sell at breakeven, which creates resistance. The 2024 cohort showed this in May, when bitcoin hit their roughly $82,100 average and then fell to $60,000 before breaking above it in August. Cost bases have acted as both floors and ceilings all cycle. The 2023 cohort's level, near $65,000, has repeatedly held as support. The 2026 cohort, at about $73,500, has been in profit since late August.

Watch two numbers: $88,000 as the ceiling and roughly $82,300 as the floor. That lower figure is the average cost of deposits into U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs, a group that only just returned to profit this year. If bitcoin falls through it, those investors may start selling too.

GE Ships Four F404 Engines to HAL as Tejas Mk-1A Deliveries Loom

07 Oct 2026

GE Aerospace said on October 7 that it shipped four F404-IN20 engines for the Tejas Mk-1A fighter jet in September 2026, bringing the total the US engine maker has delivered under HAL's 99-engine order to 14. GE previously sent three engines in August, so seven have moved in two months. India ordered 99 of the engines in 2021 for the Tejas Mk-1A programme, and GE delivered the first one on March 25, 2025. Before this 2021 order, GE says it supplied 65 F404-IN20s for earlier Tejas variants through 2016. Former HAL chairman D K Sunil said in April that GE had assured delivery of 20 engines between June and December 2026, and a press report says GE is expected to ship more than 20 engines annually from 2027.

On the aircraft side, HAL has built about 25 Tejas Mk-1A airframes that are waiting on engines, according to the Times of India. It has not delivered a single Mk-1A to the Indian Air Force so far. The contract for the first 83 aircraft, of which 73 are Mk-1A fighters and 10 are twin-seat trainers, was signed in February 2021 at ₹48,000 crore. Deliveries were meant to finish within eight years of signing, with a schedule of three aircraft in the third year and 16 per year after that. The first Mk-1A was due in March 2024. That date slipped. On October 3, Air Chief Marshal A P Singh said the induction delay is hurting the air force, whose fighter squadron count has fallen to 30 against a sanctioned 42 following the retirement of the MiG-21 fleet last year. He said the country needs to produce up to 40 aircraft a year to close capability gaps and that he is counting on the Tejas Mk-1A, the multi-role fighter tender and more Sukhoi-30MKIs to keep squadrons from falling below 30. Asked whether HAL would face penalties, he said delays happen in defence programmes but this one had been long. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh in September called for realistic timelines, warning that slippage creates gaps in meeting defence requirements, cost escalation and technology obsolescence.

Behind the engine shortage is a supply-chain problem specific to this order. Shawn Warren, GE Aerospace's general manager for combat and trainer engines, said the F404-IN20 production line had been dormant for five years when HAL placed the additional order, so GE had to restart it and re-engage its global supply chain, a process made harder by the COVID-19 pandemic. The F404-IN20 was picked for the single-engine Tejas in 2004.

Delays also came from certifications, according to the Times of India, which said the March 2024 deadline was missed for a combination of engine supply and certification timing. A second, larger contract signed in September 2025 covers 97 more Mk-1A aircraft (68 fighters and 29 twin-seaters) worth more than ₹62,370 crore excluding taxes. The ministry said deliveries under that contract begin in 2027-28 and run six years, with indigenous content above 64 percent and 67 additional items over the first contract. Combined, the two orders cover 180 jets worth about ₹1.1 lakh crore. The Times of India reports IAF and HAL are confident the first Mk-1A will enter service by year-end, and that deliveries under the 2021 contract will begin in December.

HAL's capacity to absorb engines is not the binding constraint. The company can build 24 Mk-1As a year across its Bengaluru and Nashik lines, and aims to finish the 2021 order in three to four years once parts flow.

Separately, HAL disclosed it has agreed to buy CAE Canada's remaining 50 percent of HATSOFF Helicopter Training for nil consideration, taking the joint venture to 100 percent ownership. HATSOFF runs simulator-based training for military and civil helicopter pilots, serving the air force, army, navy, coast guard, Border Security Force and civil operators including Pawan Hans and Global Vectra. It had FY26 turnover of ₹80.95 crore and paid-up capital of ₹76.80 crore. The deal covers 3,84,04,205 shares of ₹10 face value each and is expected to close within 60 days. HAL already held 50 percent, so the purchase is a related-party transaction and is not at arm's length, the filing says. HAL has administrative approval from the Ministry of Defence and concurrence from DIPAM. Because the seller's stake was acquired for nil consideration, the filing does not show a cash outflow for CAE's exit, which suggests the value was settled another way or the book value was considered negligible against the strategic control gained. The transaction, disclosed under SEBI's listing regulations, gives HAL full management control of the training unit.

What is still unknown: whether the December delivery start and year-end induction targets hold; how many of the 25 ready airframes can be completed quickly once engines arrive; whether GE's stated plan of 20 engines in calendar 2026 is met, since it needs to ship roughly 13 more in October through December after 14 delivered so far; whether HAL faces financial penalties for the missed March 2024 deadline; and the terms that left CAE accepting nil consideration for its half of HATSOFF.

Risks for a HAL shareholder: engine deliveries remain outside HAL's control and are the main brake on revenue recognition from the 2021 order; air force frustration has become public, which raises the odds of penalty clauses being invoked or future orders being structured differently; and HAL must still certify and flight-test aircraft before handover, so airframes sitting idle do not earn. HATSOFF is small next to the fighter business, with turnover under ₹81 crore against a ₹48,000 crore Tejas contract, so it matters more for control and training revenue than for earnings.

What to watch next: GE's monthly engine shipment figures, which are the single best leading indicator for HAL's Tejas revenue; confirmation of the December handover; the annual run rate GE reaches in 2027; progress on the second 97-aircraft contract and its 2027-28 start; any penalty assessment from the air force; and closure of the HATSOFF transfer within 60 days.

My view: this is a modest positive because it confirms engines are now moving in small batches, but 14 of 99 is early and the delivery schedule has already slipped by more than two years, so the cash from the Tejas programme still depends on a supplier HAL does not control. The HATSOFF purchase tidies up a small training asset without a cash cost, and will not move the earnings needle.

Naukri JobSpeak: white-collar hiring up 1% as AI roles jump 20%

07 Oct 2026

Info Edge's recruitment portal Naukri published its September 2026 JobSpeak report on 7 October 2026, and the headline number is thin: white-collar hiring rose 1 per cent year-on-year. Underneath that figure, demand split sharply by skill, sector, experience level and city.

The strongest category was artificial intelligence and machine learning, where hiring rose 20 per cent year-on-year, twenty times the overall rate. Global capability centre hiring grew 4 per cent. Inside the business process outsourcing and IT-enabled services sector, which grew 4 per cent overall, hiring by global capability centres jumped 59 per cent. Global capability centres are offshore offices that multinationals run for their own operations rather than for outside clients. Auto hiring rose 11 per cent, led by Hyderabad, Chennai and Bengaluru. Banking and financial services hiring was described as stable overall, but fresher hiring within it rose 14 per cent.

The experience breakdown matters more for Naukri's core business of matching candidates to employers. Hiring for candidates with 0 to 3 years of experience, the freshers segment, grew 1 per cent. The 4 to 7 years band fell 2 per cent. The 8 to 12 years band rose 5 per cent, the 13 to 15 years band 7 per cent, and those with more than 16 years of experience 5 per cent. Employers, in other words, paid up for seniority and specialised skills while barely adding entry-level roles.

Sector performance was mixed. Education, teaching and training fell 18 per cent, telecom 12 per cent, and hospitality 11 per cent. IT and software services declined 4 per cent even though AI and machine learning roles inside that broad category grew 20 per cent. That gap is the report's central point: a sector can shrink overall while its specialised roles expand.

City data followed the same pattern. Udaipur rose 19 per cent, Chandigarh 18 per cent, Amritsar and Ludhiana 13 per cent each, Jodhpur 12 per cent, Hyderabad 9 per cent and Lucknow 8 per cent. Bengaluru grew 4 per cent and Chennai 6 per cent. The large established markets went the other way: Delhi-NCR fell 6 per cent, Mumbai 6 per cent and Jaipur 4 per cent.

Hitesh Oberoi, managing director and chief executive of Info Edge, said the September numbers reflect a hiring market that remains steady but is becoming more selective, with demand driven by specific business and capability needs. That is the company's own characterisation of its report.

The reason for the skew is the mix of employer demand. Companies are adding people where a specific capability is scarce, chiefly AI and machine learning, and holding back where work is routine or where budgets are being reviewed. Experienced professionals are easier to justify because they can be deployed on defined projects. Freshers need training, and training costs sit awkwardly against cautious budgets. The same logic explains why global capability centres are hiring hard while broader IT services shrink: these are captive centres building in-house capability, not client-billing operations that expand with each contract won.

What remains unknown is whether this is a durable shift or a soft patch. September's 1 per cent is a single month against a year-earlier base; a low base can flatter a category such as AI, where 20 per cent growth on a small pool of jobs looks dramatic. The report does not give absolute job counts, so the reader cannot see how many postings sit behind each percentage. It also does not say whether the 4 to 7 years decline is companies cutting those roles or simply not replacing people who leave. City-level figures for Delhi-NCR and Mumbai are not broken down by sector, so it is unclear whether those declines come from IT, finance or elsewhere. The articles give no revenue, margin or billing figures for Info Edge itself.

For Naukri, the risks are straightforward. Its revenue depends on the volume of job postings and on recruiters paying to reach candidates. A market where total postings grow 1 per cent, and where the fast-growing categories are narrow, limits how much that volume can expand. A shift toward senior and specialised hiring is not automatically bad, because those placements can carry higher value and employers pay more to fill hard roles. But a 4 to 7 years decline and near-flat fresher hiring hit the widest part of the portal's traffic, and traffic is what makes the listing business work. Competition among job platforms for the same recruiter budgets is not addressed in these reports. There is also concentration risk: if AI hiring cools, the one category growing at double digits goes with it, and the underlying market is back to roughly flat.

My view is that this is modestly negative for the near-term posting volumes that drive Naukri's core business, offset partly by the higher value of specialised and senior listings. It is a signal about demand mix, not evidence of a collapse.

What to watch next: the October JobSpeak figures, to see whether AI and global capability centre growth holds or was a one-month spike; whether the 4 to 7 years band returns to growth; whether Delhi-NCR and Mumbai stop falling; and any commentary from Info Edge management on recruiter activity and paid listings when the company next reports. The fresher trend deserves particular attention, because entry-level hiring feeds the portal's future candidate base.

These discrepancies reflect the fact that final terms are still subject to change.

RIL currently owns 66.43% of Jio Platforms, or 5,937,841,645 shares, and will remain the promoter. Because the IPO is a fresh issue, RIL is not selling any shares; its stake will fall as new shares are issued, but the exact post-issue percentage is not yet known. Other major shareholders include Jaadhu Holdings (linked to Meta) with 9.98%, Google International with 7.73%, and several global investors such as Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund, Vista Equity Partners, Silver Lake, Mubadala, General Atlantic, the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, and TPG Capital, each holding between 0.93% and 2.31%. There are 94 other public shareholders holding a combined 1.75% pre-issue. No offer for sale (OFS) is included, meaning all proceeds go to the company.

Jio Platforms plans to use the net proceeds to fully or partly repay borrowings of Reliance Jio Infocomm, its material subsidiary, and for general corporate purposes. The company reported revenue from operations of Rs 1,46,885.3 crore and EBITDA of Rs 76,255.4 crore in Fiscal 2026, with an EBITDA margin of 51.91%. Revenue grew at a 15.79% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) between Fiscal 2024 and Fiscal 2026, while EBITDA grew at 17.79%. Net leverage declined from 0.88 times in Fiscal 2024 to 0.36 times in Fiscal 2026. Jio had 524.4 million customers as of March 31, 2026, making it India’s largest digital connectivity player, with a 4G and 5G subscriber base about 1.4 times that of the second-largest player. It carried around 60% of India’s wireless data traffic in Fiscal 2026 and added about 27 million net active mobility customers, roughly three times the additions of the second-largest player.

The IPO has implications for the broader telecom sector. Motilal Oswal Financial Services (MOFSL) said the listing could unlock value for RIL and drive a re-rating, as RIL’s current stock price implies an 18–36% holding-company discount on its Jio stake, depending on the valuation multiple assigned to Reliance Retail Ventures (RRVL). MOFSL values Jio Platforms at Rs 11.2 lakh crore ($117 billion), or about 12.1 times FY28 estimated EV/EBITDA (enterprise value to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization). RIL’s 66.48% stake is valued at about Rs 7.45 lakh crore, or Rs 550 per RIL share, before a 25% holding-company discount, and Rs 413 per share after. The brokerage maintains a Buy rating on RIL with a sum-of-the-parts target price of Rs 1,530 per share.

MOFSL also expects the IPO to strengthen the case for a telecom tariff hike, as delays in tariff increases have muted RIL and Bharti Airtel shares this year. It has built in a roughly 15% smartphone tariff hike in December 2026, which it expects to provide visibility for around 15% EBITDA CAGR over FY26–29. For Jio, MOFSL estimates FY28 revenue of Rs 1.69 lakh crore and EBITDA of about Rs 95,700 crore, with an EBITDA margin rising to 56.6%. The brokerage believes concerns about a shift in telecom allocations from Bharti to Jio are overblown, given Jio’s limited initial free float, and sees potential multiple re-rating for both. It values Bharti’s India business at a similar ~12 times FY28 estimated EV/EBITDA and notes Bharti’s stakes in Indus Towers and Airtel Africa are worth about Rs 1.25 trillion.

For RIL shareholders, the IPO includes a separate reservation portion, but eligibility depends on holding RIL shares on the date the Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) is filed with the Registrar of Companies. That date has not been disclosed. The reservation size is a placeholder in the draft prospectus and will be finalized in the RHP. The maximum bid under the shareholder quota is Rs 2 lakh per eligible shareholder, with allotment on a proportionate basis. There is no discount for RIL shareholders. Eligible shareholders can also apply in the retail category. The final price band, lot size, and exact reservation are still awaited.

The key unknown is the final valuation and pricing, which will depend on investor demand amid a volatile market. The National Stock Exchange recently reduced its offering size due to market conditions. Jio’s IPO has 19 banks managing the transaction, including Kotak Mahindra Capital, Morgan Stanley, BofA Securities, Axis Capital, BNP Paribas, and Citigroup. Risks include the need for continuous network upgrades, trademark and intellectual property risks (Jio does not control the use of the “Jio” trademark by other Reliance Group companies), and competitive pressures. The DRHP also notes that 2G migration and home broadband present opportunities: over 263.5 million Indians were on 2G as of March 31, 2026, and fixed broadband penetration was around 20% in Fiscal 2026, projected to reach 46% by Fiscal 2031. Jio had 27.1 million home broadband customers.

My view: The IPO is a major step for Jio Platforms, providing it with a public currency and potentially unlocking value for RIL shareholders, but the final valuation and market reception remain uncertain. The listing could also influence telecom pricing dynamics, though the timing and magnitude of any tariff hike are not guaranteed. For RIL holders, the shareholder quota offers a chance to participate, but the eligibility date and reservation details are still unknown, so monitoring the RHP filing is essential.

What to watch next: the filing of the RHP, which will set the eligibility date for the shareholder quota and disclose the final reservation size; the official IPO opening and listing dates; the final price band and valuation; and any announcements on tariff hikes from telecom operators. Additionally, watch for updates on Jio’s financial performance and any regulatory changes that could affect the telecom sector.

The signatories include former CFO Sanjay Baweja, ex-Myntra CEO Mukesh Bansal, former chief business officer Ankit Nagori, former chief technology officers Amod Malviya and Ravi Garikipati, former chief product officer Mekin Maheshwari, and former vice-president Anuj Chowdhary. The letter is addressed to Walmart chairman Gregory B Penner and other senior executives at the US retailer.

Vested options are shares an employee has earned the right to buy or sell after a waiting period; they are no longer contingent on staying. The former executives say some of their options were granted between 2008 and 2016 and have sat for more than a decade. Their argument, as stated in the letter, is that leaving Flipkart should not by itself disqualify them from a liquidity event, because the equity was part of the pay they accepted while building the company.

The sums involved are large. More than 30,000 current and former employees hold roughly $4 billion, or Rs 38,000 crore, in ESOP wealth that Walmart has not yet turned into cash, sources told Moneycontrol. Former employees account for about half of that, with current staff holding the other $2 billion. Moneycontrol says the figures come from people familiar with the situation, not from Walmart or Flipkart.

The timing matters. Walmart is understood to be preparing another liquidity programme for current employees, and the former executives want to be included in it. Flipkart has run several buybacks before. Including the most recent exercise in July this year, the company has generated more than $1.5 billion in value for employees, Moneycontrol reported earlier, at a time when Flipkart's valuation touched $38 billion. The former staff are not disputing those past programmes; they say the next one should be open to them too.

The letter also draws a comparison with other holders. Early investors Accel and Tiger Global and co-founder Binny Bansal have already exited or realised large gains, Moneycontrol has reported: Accel made cumulative returns of about $1.5 billion to $2 billion on an investment of roughly $60 million to $80 million, a 25 to 30 times return; Tiger Global made about $3.5 billion; Binny Bansal made about $1 billion to $1.5 billion between Flipkart's founding in 2007 and his exit in 2023. The former executives say they respect those returns and are not asking for preferential treatment, only for what they call fair and equitable treatment of equity they earned.

The dispute lands while Flipkart's plans for an initial public offering, a stock market listing, remain unclear. Moneycontrol reported a day before the letter that morale among current employees is sagging over the IPO delay and the contrast with peers such as Snapdeal, Meesho, Groww and Swiggy, which have listed and put money in employees' hands. At least 10 executives at vice-president and senior vice-president level have left Flipkart in recent months, with the lack of clarity on the IPO and stock options cited as concerns. Walmart has not publicly responded to the letter, and it is not known whether the board will extend the buyback to former staff, how eligibility would be defined, or on what timetable.

Separately, Flipkart's Big Billion Days festive sale opens with early access on October 8 and general sale on October 9, running against Amazon's Great Indian Festival, which also starts at midnight. The advertised smartphone deals show how much margin is being spent to pull buyers in. Flipkart lists the 256GB iPhone 17 at Rs 84,999 during the sale, against a launch price of Rs 99,900; with a Rs 2,000 bank discount and a Rs 3,000 exchange bonus, the effective price falls to Rs 79,999, a total benefit of Rs 19,901. One source gives a slightly different headline figure, teasing the iPhone 17 at Rs 76,999, so the exact entry price depends on which offers a buyer qualifies for. Flipkart is offering 10% instant discount with Axis and ICICI Bank cards; Amazon is offering 10% with SBI debit and credit cards. Other Flipkart sale prices include the Poco X8 Pro Max 5G from Rs 42,999, the Mivi One 5G from Rs 11,999, the Realme P4 5G at Rs 29,999, the Moto Edge 70 Fusion from Rs 29,999 and the Vivo T5x from Rs 24,999. Flipkart is also pushing appliance exchange, a category where customers tend to repair rather than replace. Its Big Billion Days film, made with the agency 22feet, imagines a retirement home for old appliances, and the campaign ran an on-ground activation in Mumbai on October 3 and 4. During the sale, buyers get an exchange bonus on top of the trade-in value of an old appliance. A second campaign item, a social-first BBD campaign via SW Network, appears in the source list only as a headline with no usable detail.

My view: the ESOP letter is a governance and retention problem more than a cash problem for Walmart, which can fund a $4 billion buyback if it chooses. But it arrives at the worst moment, with senior staff leaving and no IPO date, and it puts a number on the employee wealth that has been promised but not paid. The sale discounts are the ordinary cost of the festive quarter and say little about the year ahead.

What to watch: whether Walmart's board answers the October 1 letter and whether the next liquidity programme includes former employees; whether more senior exits follow; any signal on the IPO timetable; and how Flipkart's festive sale volumes and discount levels compare with Amazon's once the sale closes.
