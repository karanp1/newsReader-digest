- Analysis: Why NASA wants to build a nuclear reactor on the moon
  07 Oct 2026 | https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/science/analysis-why-nasa-wants-to-build-nuclear-reactor-on-the-moon/article71552807.ece
  NASA plans a 20 kW nuclear reactor near the moon's south pole by 2030, enough for a few households on Earth. Russia and China's ILRS reactor would produce about 10 kW. The south pole draws attention because craters there hold water ice, which can be electrolysed into hydrogen and oxygen for rocket propellant. NASA scientists estimated 10 tonnes of oxygen would need roughly 68 kW, so 20 kW is a stepping stone toward larger reactor arrays. The Outer Space Treaty bars territorial claims, but Artemis Accords safety zones could let a country cluster reactors and limit rivals' access. Watch the technical hurdles: shielding, waste-heat radiators, and moondust.

  06 Oct 2026 | https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-06/ice-brings-futures-trading-londons-190-billion-day-physical-gold-market
  ICE, the exchange group that owns the New York Stock Exchange, launched precious metals futures trading in London this week, with contracts tied directly to the city's daily gold price auctions. Silver, platinum and palladium futures launched alongside. London is the world's dominant physical gold market, handling roughly $190 billion in daily over-the-counter trades, with about $1.4 trillion in bullion sitting in its vaults. Futures are standardized contracts to buy or sell metal at a set price later, and until now traders wanting that kind of exchange-traded exposure had to go to New York. ¶ The gap was odd: London dominates physical trading but has lacked a major futures market for years. Two earlier attempts died, the London Gold Futures Market in 1985 and the London Metal Exchange's contract in 2022, both for lack of trading volume. ICE's edge is that it already runs the daily auctions that set benchmark prices, so it can link contracts to a benchmark it controls. ¶ The bet is that a futures market can only survive where the pricing power already sits, and this time it does. Watch whether trading volume builds in the first year, since that is exactly what killed the last two tries.
