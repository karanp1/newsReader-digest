Thailand approves bitcoin and ether ETF rules from Oct. 16

09 Oct 2026

Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission issued rules allowing bitcoin and ether exchange-traded funds, effective Oct. 16. The funds must track crypto asset prices and hold average net exposure of at least 80% of net asset value to that asset over each accounting year. They must use SEC-licensed custodians and trade only on the Stock Exchange of Thailand.

Investors will have to acknowledge crypto ETF risks before trading, and securities firms cannot offer margin loans to finance purchases. Asset managers can outsource only to licensed digital asset fund managers. Qualified digital asset firms can supervise crypto ETFs if they meet requirements for financial standing, staffing and operational systems.

Thai mutual funds and private funds can now buy locally listed crypto ETFs. Previously they could invest only in foreign crypto ETFs, though existing investment limits still apply. The regulator also barred depositary receipts linked to foreign crypto ETFs in the initial phase, and securities firms cannot arrange foreign crypto ETF investments for clients outside the institutional and ultra-high-net-worth categories.

For bitcoin holders, this is a small but concrete step: a new regulated onshore route for Thai institutions and wealthy individuals to get exposure. It does not change global flows, and the 80% exposure rule plus the margin ban keep the structure conservative.
