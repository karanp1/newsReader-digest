IT sector sees an uptick in fresher hiring after two years of slowdown

India’s $300 billion technology and

IT services industry is seeing a revival in fresher onboarding after nearly two years of subdued hiring.

Even so, entry-level recruitment remains below mid-level hiring as demand for experienced AI talent stays strong.

Number wise: Fresher hiring, which fell to a record low of around 110,000 in FY26, is expected to rise to 115,000-135,000 in the current fiscal year, according to recruitment firm Quess Group. That implies a 4.5-22.7% increase.

The decline had been steady:

FY22: 380,000 FY23: 250,000

FY24: 155,000 FY25: 125,000

FY26: ~110,000 A rebound began in the June quarter, with TCS, Infosys and HCLTech together recruiting about 19,000 entry-level staff, Nasscom said in its September quarter report covering 29 IT services firms.

Higher bar for freshers: Experts told us the bar for entry-level talent has risen. Firms increasingly want specialised capabilities in AI, cloud, data and cybersecurity to improve revenue visibility, meet client demands and ensure deployment readiness with limited ramp-up time.

Bitcoin, Ethereum, XRP Pare Earlier Gains as Sentiment Turns Negative

02 Oct 2026

Bitcoin sold off to about $84,600 after a Friday morning rally to $86,500, and Ethereum and XRP followed it down. The trigger was a weaker-than-expected jobs report, which is the monthly US hiring data that sets the tone for riskier assets. Crypto trades like a risk asset, so a soft report can spook holders fast.

What stands out is the mood. Social sentiment tracker Santiment says Ethereum is at 0.89 bullish comments for every bearish one, its lowest since June 7, and XRP is at 0.67, its weakest since Aug. 17. A ratio below 1.0 means pessimism dominates. Santiment reads that as a contrarian signal: when most traders already expect a fall, much of the panic selling may already have happened. It is a lean, not a guarantee.

The money flows disagree with each other. XRP spot ETFs, funds that hold the token for investors, took in $4.07 million on Oct. 1, ending two flat sessions. Ethereum ETFs lost $55.37 million that day, a third straight outflow, totaling $117.76 million.

Watch whether ETF flows stabilize. If they do, the sour sentiment is the more useful signal.

Bitcoin Investors Who Bought Last Year's Rally Are Selling As Price Nears Their Break-Even, Glassnode Says

03 Oct 2026

Bitcoin buyers from last year's rally are now selling at the fastest daily pace of any point this year, according to on-chain analytics firm Glassnode. These are investors who bought six to 24 months ago and are sitting on losses. Their average purchase prices were roughly $89,000 to $97,000, while Bitcoin traded around $84,673 on Saturday, meaning the six-to-12-month group needs about a 5% gain to break even and the one-to-two-year group needs nearly 15%.

That gap explains the selling. Investors holding at a loss often wait for the price to recover to what they paid, then exit without losing money. Each average purchase price becomes a level where selling pressure builds. Glassnode's chart showed Bitcoin approaching those levels twice and turning lower both times. Meanwhile, holders who bought during the decline are not selling, so the supply hitting the market is concentrated in one group rather than spread across all holders. Retail sentiment on Stocktwits shifted to bearish.

Watch whether Bitcoin can push through those break-even levels. If it cannot, the same cohort will likely keep capping every rally.

Gold’s resilience supports strong outlook, silver faces oversupply, copper could be the big gainer – Deutsche Bank’s Ghali

02 Oct 2026

Gold has held up better than it should have. Despite US 10-year yields above 5% and crude oil above $100 a barrel, gold has not printed a new low since July, and Daniel Ghali, Deutsche Bank's head of metals research, calls that resilient price action. The reason is positioning: investors are unusually bearish on gold, with the most bearish setup since October 2021. Official sector purchases are running at more than double their 2022 pace, the pool of institutional gold investors has grown roughly 70% since 2021, and reserve managers have more money to allocate. Ghali argues the bear market in Treasuries is itself pushing pension funds and insurers to diversify, which supports gold rather than competing with it.

Silver is the opposite story. London vault inventories available for purchase are back at their highest since November 2024, Chinese industrial demand for solar silver is down about a third in 2026, and Ghali sees a possible physical surplus. He expects silver to underperform gold with less volatility. Copper is his pick for the biggest gains, with the tightest supply since the 1980s: the US and China have locked up roughly 70% of the world's aboveground inventory. Watch copper inventories, not Fed headlines, for the next real move.

OpenAI/Anthropic/Kimi/Deepseek/OpenRouter

The eternal complement

03 Oct 2026

Humanity can see almost to the beginning of time but has never sent a person past the Moon, and that gap between what we can imagine and what we can build is widening. The essay's evidence is economic: sustaining Moore's law, the pattern of chips doubling in power every couple of years, now takes more than eighteen times as many researchers as it did in the early 1970s. Across the US economy, research effort rose twenty-three-fold from the 1930s while research productivity fell by a factor of forty-one.

The reason is that ideas and the machinery to execute them are complements, meaning more of one raises the value of the other. A better telescope makes a good astronomy question more valuable, and vice versa. The James Webb Space Telescope needed three hundred organizations across fourteen countries, not just smarter physicists. The author calls this unglamorous execution layer "institutional intelligence" and argues AI is starting to supply it, writing code and turning sketches into prototypes so one person can pursue an idea that once required an organization. My view: the interesting question is not whether AI can generate ideas, since it already can, but whether it gets pointed at the quarries and the accounting, because that is where the bottleneck actually sits.

The thinking is that monsoon misery looks different depending on where you live, so a single national ad would miss the point. Out-of-home advertising, meaning billboards and bus branding, is usually a blunt, one-message medium. Here it is doing the opposite: the executions change city by city, and the towel's real texture and colours appear on buses and hoardings rather than a generic product shot. Welspun's domestic business CEO, Ruchika Arora, said the goal was to make the benefit visible rather than just state it.

The bet is that a damp towel is a strong enough everyday annoyance to carry a premium product, though it is fair to ask whether a clever hoarding sells towels or just wins the campaign a mention. Watch whether the product claim, not the stunt, ends up in the recall.
