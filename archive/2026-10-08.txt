### Quick commerce fuels festive credit demand as brands seek working capital
08 Oct 2026 | https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/newsletters/morning-dispatch/startup-of-the-year-groww-bets-on-scale-qcomm-sparks-festive-credit-surge/articleshow/134777622.cms

India's quick-commerce boom is giving festive-season credit demand a fresh leg, with D2C brands borrowing more to stock inventory in dark stores as the channel gains a larger share of online sales this year.

Brands scaling on quick commerce typically need to maintain 1.7-2.5X inventory, compared with 1-1.25X for traditional ecommerce. For instance, D2C perfume brand Secret Alchemist saw its working capital requirement rise 35% year-on-year this festive season. Indifi has seen disbursements to quick-commerce-focused sellers rise 50% year-on-year, while FlexiLoans expects festive disbursements to grow 40-50% to more than Rs 1,000 crore. Food and snacking brands are particularly dependent on the channel, with quick commerce accounting for 50-70% of sales for some brands in Recur Club's portfolio.

Quick commerce is expected to account for 16% of festive spending this year, up from 12% in 2025, according to Datum Intelligence. The compressed four-month festive calendar is also concentrating borrowing needs as brands stock up earlier and replenish inventory faster. Watch whether lenders keep up with the higher inventory intensity as the channel takes a bigger share of sales.

### MeitY planning strict protocol for swift takedown of CSEAM content

The Centre will soon notify a standard operating procedure (SOP) to help online users report child sexual exploitation and abuse material (CSEAM) and facilitate its removal, officials said.

The SOP, modelled on a similar protocol for non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII), is expected to be created by next week. The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCCRP) may also create a separate category for reporting CSEAM. The SOP will spell out protocols for victims, intermediaries and law enforcement agencies to report, remove and prevent the spread of such content. Officials said the SOP is part of a broader effort to strengthen the legal framework governing individuals' online rights, amid growing concerns over social media harms such as harassment and the sharing of non-consensual intimate images.

Last November, the Centre launched an SOP to curb NCII, along with a tighter removal deadline. As much as 85% of such content is now removed by social media intermediaries within two hours of being flagged, MeitY officials said. Earlier this year, the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) and the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) began enquiries into advertisements of child abuse content on Meta platforms in India. Subsequently, Meta and Google announced they will proactively report all CSEAM detected on their platforms to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C). Watch how quickly platforms comply with the new reporting category.

### Global pick: OpenAI is pissing off a bunch of mathematicians—again
Wired | https://www.wired.com/story/openai-is-pissing-off-a-bunch-of-mathematicians-again/

OpenAI plans to dump hundreds of AI-generated solutions to unsolved math problems onto GitHub on Tuesday, according to people familiar with the plans who spoke to WIRED. The release follows a pattern that has left many mathematicians angry: the company announces results by blog post or tweet rather than through papers that can be checked and credited.

The context matters. In August, OpenAI gathered around 40 mathematicians and, attendees say, hinted its models had cracked hundreds of long-standing problems. The group asked the company to publish papers explaining the work so the field could absorb it. Northwestern's Bryna Kra says that input was apparently ignored. OpenAI spokesperson Lindsay McCallum denies the company made the no-bulk-release assurance attendees recall, and says no release time has been set.

The deeper fight is about credit and speed. In September, NYU's Tristan Buckmaster accused OpenAI of racing to solve a Millennium Prize problem, worth $1 million, after hearing rivals were close, and of trying to exclude his collaborator from a paper. OpenAI denies the characterization. Mathematicians describe the field as a showcase for OpenAI and Anthropic as both approach public offerings, with normal scientific process set aside. My view: the tools are real, but the labs are burning the goodwill of the people best placed to verify them, and that will cost more than any single release.

### Global pick: South Korea’s AI chip giants are fighting for a bigger role in America’s AI boom
Rest of World | https://restofworld.org/2026/samsung-sk-hynix-ai-memory-chip-war-nvidia-openai/

Samsung has more than doubled its share of the high-bandwidth memory market in the past year, closing on SK Hynix, which had long led. The two South Korean firms together control 83% of global memory chip production. High-bandwidth memory is the specialized memory that lets AI processors pull huge amounts of data fast, so it sits inside nearly every system used to train and run AI models.

SK Hynix got its lead through more advanced core technology and an early supply relationship with Nvidia. Samsung has spent the past year signing its own deals to catch up: work with Nvidia on AI-RAN (embedding AI in cellular base stations) in March 2025, joining OpenAI's Stargate infrastructure project that October, an AI factory with Nvidia, and ChatGPT Enterprise rolled out to its employees. It is also working with OpenAI on next-generation chips. SK Hynix is building a $4 billion advanced packaging facility in Indiana.

For Nvidia and OpenAI, the contest matters because who supplies this component affects how fast and how cheaply they can build the next wave of AI infrastructure. Analysts say Nvidia's allocation decisions still determine market share more than anything else, and Samsung's real test is whether it can take supply share for Nvidia's Vera Rubin. The complication is China, roughly a third of the global market, where both firms have legacy plants and now face annual U.S. licensing plus rising domestic Chinese rivals. My view: watch Samsung's Vera Rubin allocation, since that single decision will show whether this is a real shift in leadership or just a stronger second place.
