Ecommerce firms scale logistics, hiring ahead of festive sales

As the festive sales draw closer, ecommerce giants Amazon and Flipkart

are significantly expanding their supply-chain networks.

Driving the news: The companies are doing this through additional warehousing, more delivery hubs, faster delivery options and deeper penetration into tier‑II and tier‑III cities.

Flipkart has added 14 million cubic feet of warehousing space, lifting storage capacity by 50%, and added over 900 last‑mile delivery hubs.

Ekart and Flipkart Minutes now operate more than 8,200 facilities, extending reach to locations such as Leh and Kargil for the first time during Big Billion Days.

Amazon has launched 20 new fulfilment centres and other logistics units in cities including Raipur, Ranchi, Hassan, Meerut and Varanasi, plus six new sort centres and 150 new last‑mile delivery stations.

Now hiring: Amazon and Flipkart are expected to hire over 410,000 temporary workers across direct and indirect roles, about 30,000 more than last year.

Meesho plans to create over one million seasonal opportunities, including around 6.5 lakh across its seller network and 3.75 lakh in logistics.

Industry estimates project festive season ecommerce sales of Rs 1.50-1.55 lakh crore,

up 25-29% year-on-year . Quick commerce’s share of that is expected to rise to 16% at about Rs 24,000 crore.

Vertex in funding talks with RapidCanvas, Supernova to sharpen AI focus: Sources

Rahul Pangam and Uttam Phalnikar, founders, RapidCanvas

Vertex Ventures is in talks to back RapidCanvas and Supernova, sharpening its AI focus after consumer investments in India, people aware of the matter said.

Deal details: Vertex is likely to invest about $20 million in Hyderabad- and Austin-based RapidCanvas’ $20-30 million round at a $120 million post-money valuation.

Existing investors are expected to participate, while a new investor may also come on board, the people added.

It is also discussing a $15-20 million investment alongside Verlinvest in Bengaluru-based Supernova at a $100 million post-money valuation.

Why it matters: The proposed bets target businesses using AI to lower service costs. RapidCanvas automates enterprise data work, while Supernova offers speaking practice without live tutors.

Track record: Vertex, typically a consumer internet-focused investor, recently led rounds in creator-commerce platform Wishlink and food brand Anveshan . The proposed deals signal a sharper AI focus for Vertex, in line with a broader shift among venture capital firms globally.

Arivihan raises $10 million from Prosus, Accel to expand AI tutoring

Sonu Kumar Prashant, Ritesh Singh Chandel and Rushabh Kothari, founders, Arivihan

Edtech startup Arivihan has raised $10 million in a round led by existing investors Prosus and Accel. The round values the startup at Rs 570 crore, as per filings.

Details: The Indore-based startup aims to expand its artificial intelligence (AI) tutoring services across state boards and competitive examinations.

Cofounder and chief executive Ritesh Singh Chandel told ET that Arivihan would use the funds to cover almost all state boards by year-end, expand its Central Board of Secondary Education offering and deepen its presence in medical entrance preparation.

Kitchen appliance startup EDT raises $2.4 million in funding led by Sauce VC

Mumbai-based kitchen appliance brand EDT

has raised $2.4 million in a round led by existing investor Sauce, as the company builds inventory ahead of the festive season and expands into beauty gadgets.

New trend: This comes as investors increasingly look to back modern kitchen and cookware brands. Rising incomes and changing consumer preferences are driving demand for products that promise not just functionality, but also safer materials and sharper design.

ETtech Explainer: Multi-billion dollar global battle for AI control

Frontier AI companies are moving nearer to the chips that power their models, while chipmakers are moving up into the model layer.

Why the trend: Chipmakers are making this move to understand how AI models are evolving because those models determine what future computing systems need to do.

AMD’s $8.2 billion acquisition of World Labs is an example of this. AMD said the deal will help shape its hardware, software and systems roadmaps by aligning them more closely with real-world model demands.

Why chips: For AI companies, soaring compute needs are driving up costs and creating incentives to customise silicon. Additionally, owning more layers of the stack allows tighter optimisation across hardware, models and software.

State of anticipation: on Jammu and Kashmir and Statehood

30 Sep 2026

https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/state-of-anticipation/article71524628.ece

In a resolution passed on September 28 , the Jammu and Kashmir Assembly called upon the Government of India for the “immediate and urgent restoration of full Statehood” to the nearly seven-year-old Union Territory (UT). The erstwhile State of J&K was downgraded and carved out into two UTs in 2019 when the Centre decided to end the region’s close to 70 years of special constitutional status. Chief Minister Omar Abdullah moved the resolution with a reminder of the past resolutions adopted by the Assembly over the demand of special status. It mentioned the greater autonomy resolution of June 26, 2000, and the special status resolution of November 6, 2024. Other than the 29 BJP legislators , the members of the 90-member House broadly supported the resolution after an intense and high-pitched debate on Statehood and special status. It was a rare bid by Mr. Abdullah on Statehood since he won the election two years ago. Both the ruling alliance parties, the Congress, CPI(M) and Independents, as well as the Opposition parties, including the Peoples Democratic Party, J&K Peoples Conference, Aam Aadmi Party and Awami Ittehad Party extended their support to the demand. The Centre itself laid down a three-step path for the restoration of Statehood: delimitation, election and Statehood. The Supreme Court of India had sought the restoration of Statehood “as soon as possible”, while Prime Minister Narendra Modi had promised the same “at the appropriate time”, both in Parliament and outside it. Still, the wait has been too long for not only the people of J&K, but for all those who value democracy and federalism.

Ideological positions aside, the downgrading of J&K from a full-fledged functional State to a UT was unprecedented and unhealthy. No doubt, J&K went through three decades of tumultuous times, as militant violence and deaths became the order of the day. However, the 2024 elections were an unprecedented departure from the past. Voters in Kashmir shunned boycott politics and J&K recorded 63.88% voting. It was the most peaceful election with not a bullet fired, a first in the past three decades, The Centre should have reciprocated the people’s faith in the democratic set-up by empowering the legislature they voted for. The first step in that direction is the restoration of Statehood. The scarred and painful past of J&K needs healing. It is high time that the Court’s direction on Statehood be respected and implemented without delay. An empowered legislature rather than the streets, should become the space for conversation with the Centre on J&K. J&K should not be allowed to drift away from the federal and democratic process in India. To predicate restoration of Statehood on the end of militancy is, as Mr. Abdullah indicated, equivalent to giving Pakistan and its strategy of state-sponsored terrorism a say in the matter.
