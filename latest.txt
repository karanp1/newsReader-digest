### Beyond gullibility: On digital arrest scams
31 Oct 2026 | https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/beyond-gullibility-on-digital-arrest-scams/article71646642.ece

• About ₹800 crore has been lost to digital arrest scams this year even after recoveries, Chief Justice Surya Kant noted at the Supreme Court's October 29 hearing.

• The Court pressed the Reserve Bank of India to finalise its KYC Amendment Directions, 2026, requiring banks to flag suspected mule accounts and place temporary debit holds.

• The Attorney-General assured the directions will be in force by December 31, and the Court insisted banks compensate victims, since the RBI's 2017 zero-liability circular covers only unauthorised transactions.

• The RBI directions can hold any unusual transaction of ₹1,000 or more for up to 60 days, which may disproportionately freeze small traders and Jan Dhan account holders who unknowingly received mule transfers.

• The editorial argues the scams thrive because victims fear high-handed police, and that India must pressure Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos, where trafficked Indians run scam compounds with some official patronage.

### Suboptimal outcomes: On social security boards for unorganised, gig, platform workers
30 Oct 2026 | https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/suboptimal-outcomes-on-social-security-boards-for-unorganised-gig-platform-workers/article71646647.ece

• The Union Labour Ministry has constituted National Social Security Boards for unorganised workers and for gig and platform workers, a year after the Code on Social Security took effect.

• Union Labour Minister Mansukh Mandaviya will chair both boards, and the Union Government nominates all members.

• The unorganised workers board has 40 members, including seven worker and seven employer representatives; the gig and platform board has 22 members, with five each from aggregators and workers.

• The newspaper argues the Union Government should include trade unions and State Governments in creating social security mechanisms for the vast majority of workers.

### Open to investigation: On the challenge to the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
30 Oct 2026 | https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/open-to-investigation-on-the-challenge-to-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023/article71641118.ece

• The Digital News Publishers Association has challenged the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and its Rules in the Supreme Court.

• The Act lacks a journalistic exemption that appeared in the 2018, 2019 and 2021 drafts, so reporters could be treated as data fiduciaries.

• Non-compliance can attract penalties of up to ₹250 crore, an existential threat to smaller and independent newsrooms.

• The petition also challenges Section 44(3), which amended the RTI Act, 2005, removing the public-interest qualification for disclosing personal information.
