  GE Aerospace shipped four F404-IN20 engines to Hindustan Aeronautics Limited in September 2026, the US engine maker said on 7 October. The F404-IN20 is the engine that powers the Tejas Light Combat Aircraft Mk-1A, the fighter HAL builds for the Indian Air Force. GE shipped three more F404 engines in August, so the two-month total is seven, according to Source 2 and Source 4. Source 5 says HAL has now received 10 engines in all, counting the September batch; Source 2 says the same, adding that GE's contract covers 99 engines. GE is expected to deliver more than 20 engines a year from 2027, Source 5 reports. ¶ The engine count matters because it sets HAL's assembly pace. At least 25 Tejas airframes are finished and waiting for engines, Source 2 says. HAL has not delivered a single LCA Mk-1A fighter to the IAF. The first aircraft, ordered in February 2021, was due in March 2024. That date slipped because GE could not supply the F404-IN20 on time and because key certifications took longer than planned, according to Source 5. The 2021 contract is worth ₹48,000 crore for 83 aircraft: 73 Mk-1A fighters and 10 Mk-1 trainers, with delivery of all 83 to be completed within eight years of signing. The plan announced in 2021 was for three aircraft in the third year and 16 a year for the following five years. A second order for 97 more Mk-1A jets, signed in September 2025, brings the IAF's total to 180 aircraft worth a combined ₹1.1 lakh crore. Deliveries under the second contract are expected to start in 2027-28 and run six years. ¶ HAL's chairman, Ravi Kota, said after completing the Tejas Mk-1 trainer contract on 18 September 2026 that he expects to deliver Mk-1A aircraft this year, according to Source 3. Jefferies, a brokerage, wrote that with 10 engines in hand there is confidence HAL can deliver 10 aircraft this year, against the brokerage's earlier assumption of five. Jefferies rates the stock Buy with a target of ₹6,800 a share; that is the brokerage's view, not the company's. The Mk-1A accounts for 43 per cent of HAL's order book, Source 3 says, and the stock has de-rated since calendar 2024 over the delays. Analysts quoted in Source 3 say the IAF accepting even one aircraft would lift confidence in HAL's execution. ¶ Separately, HAL's Koraput division in Odisha delivered its 2,000th aero-engine on 6 October, six decades after the facility opened in 1964, Source 6 reports. The plant has built engines for the MiG-21, MiG-27, MiG-29 and Su-30MKI and has overhauled more than 9,000 engines. At the event, Defence Minister Rajnath Singh announced a second production line for Shakti engines at Koraput, costing ₹218 crore. The Shakti engine, developed with France's Safran Helicopter Engines, powers the Dhruv Advanced Light Helicopter and the Prachand Light Combat Helicopter. The new line will lift capacity to as many as 100 engines a year, with full establishment targeted for 2028-29. Singh also said HAL will raise annual AL-31FP engine capacity from 30 to 50 at a cost of ₹335 crore, supporting the Su-30MKI fleet after a 2024 government order for 240 of those engines. ¶ The Shakti line addresses a different bottleneck: helicopters. HAL's order backlog includes 25 Dhruv Advanced Light Helicopters, 12 Light Utility Helicopters and 156 Prachand Light Combat Helicopters, plus 10 Dhruv NG helicopters ordered by Pawan Hans, according to ICICI Securities in Source 3. A further 110 LUH and 95 LCH helicopters have been planned but not yet contracted. HAL is also expanding capacity across the LCA, HTT-40 trainer and LCH lines, with about ₹12,000 crore of manufacturing investment planned by 2030, the brokerage says. HAL can build 24 LCA Mk-1As a year at its Bengaluru and Nashik lines, Source 5 reports, and aims to finish the 2021 order in three to four years. ¶ The pressure is not only commercial. Air Chief Marshal A.P. Singh said on 3 October that the delay in inducting new fighters is hurting the IAF as its squadron strength falls, and that India needs to build up to 40 aircraft a year. He is counting on the Mk-1A, a new multi-role fighter and extra Sukhoi-30MKIs to keep the squadron count from dropping below 30, Source 5 says. Defence Minister Singh, visiting Bengaluru in September, called for realistic project timelines, warning that delays create gaps in critical defence requirements, drive cost escalation and raise the risk of technology obsolescence, Source 2 reports. The Mk-1A is meant to replace the MiG-21, retired last year after more than six decades. ¶ What remains unconfirmed is the delivery date. Sources 2 and 5 say the IAF and HAL expect the Mk-1A to enter service by year-end, with deliveries likely to begin in December, but no aircraft has been handed over yet and the schedule has slipped before. GE has not said how many engines it will ship in the coming months beyond the 20-plus annual figure from 2027. The second Shakti line is announced, not built; full capacity is targeted for 2028-29. The 110 LUH and 95 LCH orders are planned, not signed. ¶ The main risks are execution and dependence. HAL's fighter output depends on a single foreign engine supplier, and any further slip in F404 deliveries pushes revenue and cash collection to the right. The order book is large and the demand from the IAF is clear, but the gap between orders and deliveries is what has weighed on the stock. Certification and supply-chain steps outside HAL's control, including avionics, radars and electronic warfare systems, add further points of failure. ¶ My view: the engine shipments are the first concrete sign that the Mk-1A bottleneck is easing, and the Shakti line is a sensible, modest fix for a separate helicopter constraint. Neither changes the fact that HAL's problem has been converting a record order book into delivered aircraft, and one quarter of engine arrivals does not settle that. ¶ What to watch next: whether HAL hands over the first Mk-1A to the IAF by December and how many follow in the financial year; GE's monthly shipment numbers; progress on the Koraput Shakti line and the AL-31FP expansion; and any movement on the planned 110 LUH and 95 LCH contracts.
