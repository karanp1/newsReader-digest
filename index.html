OpenAIOpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo

23 Nov 2026

  • OpenAI and Cisco are launching Shirudo, an open-source runtime that protects Linux systems while teams roll out patches.
  • In a controlled test, a Codex-generated shield blocked a Java4Shell exploit against a running Elasticsearch service without a restart.
  • Shirudo uses eBPF, a Linux technology that runs small programs inside the operating system to observe activity and apply targeted protections.

Fatal disagreement: on the COP31 summit

23 Nov 2026

https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/fatal-disagreement-on-the-cop31-summit/article71735124.ece

  • COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye ended with nearly 190 countries failing to agree on a cover text after almost 12 days of talks, running close to 50 hours over.
  • The proposed cover text sought a target of 35% of global final energy consumption from electricity by 2035 to cut direct fossil fuel emissions.
  • The EU blocked the text because it lacked any explicit mention of fossil fuel, let alone a phase-out, and about 130 countries also demanded fossil fuel language.
  • COP31 did advance the Just Transition agreement but made no progress on climate finance, the other major prong of negotiations.
  • The article argues developed countries must honor past climate finance promises, and that without good-faith dialogue COP risks becoming a sideshow and a blow to multilateralism.

Just verdict: on Myanmar and the International Court of Justice

23 Nov 2026

https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/just-verdict-on-myanmar-and-the-international-court-of-justice/article71735482.ece

  • The International Court of Justice ruled on November 19 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time it has held any state responsible for the crime.
  • The verdict came by nine votes to six, with the court requiring genocide intent to be the only reasonable inference from the facts.
  • The court accepted that in 2017 at least 10,000 Rohingya were killed, 1,834 raped and 392 villages destroyed in northern Rakhine State, with over 7,00,000 fleeing to Bangladesh.
  • Myanmar must allow safe, voluntary and dignified return and pay compensation, but the court cannot enforce its judgment and only the UN Security Council can, where China and Russia hold vetoes.
  • The article argues only a truly democratic, federal, plural and peaceful Myanmar can bring the Rohingya home in safety.

What the numbers say

Tata Steel Ltd (TATASTEEL, -8.43%)

What the sector does
  • Steelmakers smelt iron ore and coal into flat and long products sold to construction, auto, appliance and infrastructure buyers, mostly through distributors and long-term contracts.
  • Revenue tracks global and domestic steel prices and volumes, while cost is dominated by iron ore, coking coal and energy, so margins swing with the commodity cycle.
What the company does
  • Tata Steel is Asia's first integrated private steel company, founded in 1907, spanning mining and processing of iron ore and coal through to finished steel distribution.
  • It sells flat and long steel products to construction, automotive and industrial customers, and had a target to raise domestic steelmaking capacity to 30 million tonnes per annum by 2025.
Why it moved today
  • Tata Steel fell 8.43% after announcing it will acquire up to 26.38% of APL Apollo Tubes from promoter S Gupta Holding for about ₹15,511 crore, triggering a mandatory open offer for another 26% at ₹2,301.40 a share.
  • The combined outlay could reach ₹32,266 crore, and a ₹5,825-crore block deal of 2.77 crore APL Apollo shares at ₹2,100 apiece followed the announcement, with APL Apollo closing down 21.8%.
The numbers (Screener)
  • The stock trades at a P/E of 17.1 and 1.96 times its book value of ₹83.1, with a dividend yield of 2.45%, ROCE of 12.5% and ROE of 11.7%.
  • September 2026 quarter sales were ₹65,436 crore with net profit of ₹3,700 crore and EPS of ₹2.94, against ₹60,794 crore and ₹2,385 crore in the June 2026 quarter.
What the numbers say
  • At a P/E of 17.1, Tata Steel sits in line with the peer median of 17.14 and below JSW Steel's 20.44, while its 12.5% ROCE beats JSW Steel's 10.96% and the peer median of 9.7%.
  • September quarter sales rose 11.5% and net profit 1.11% year on year, slower than JSW Steel's 20.67% sales growth, so the 8.43% fall reflects the APL Apollo deal rather than a results miss.
