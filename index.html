IT sector sees an uptick in fresher hiring after two years of slowdown

India’s $300 billion technology and

IT services industry is seeing a revival in fresher onboarding after nearly two years of subdued hiring.

Even so, entry-level recruitment remains below mid-level hiring as demand for experienced AI talent stays strong.

Number wise: Fresher hiring, which fell to a record low of around 110,000 in FY26, is expected to rise to 115,000-135,000 in the current fiscal year, according to recruitment firm Quess Group. That implies a 4.5-22.7% increase.

The decline had been steady:

FY22: 380,000 FY23: 250,000

FY24: 155,000 FY25: 125,000

FY26: ~110,000 A rebound began in the June quarter, with TCS, Infosys and HCLTech together recruiting about 19,000 entry-level staff, Nasscom said in its September quarter report covering 29 IT services firms.

Higher bar for freshers: Experts told us the bar for entry-level talent has risen. Firms increasingly want specialised capabilities in AI, cloud, data and cybersecurity to improve revenue visibility, meet client demands and ensure deployment readiness with limited ramp-up time.

Gold Price Canada Today | Live Gold Price in CAD

04 Oct 2026

Gold in Canada costs C$5,899.42 a troy ounce as of October 4, 2026, 4:20 a.m. EDT. That headline number is really two prices stacked together. The metal trades in U.S. dollars on the global spot market around the clock, and the USD/CAD exchange rate converts it into Canadian dollars. So a day when gold rises in U.S. terms but the loonie strengthens can leave the Canadian price nearly flat, and a weakening Canadian dollar can push the CAD price up even when spot gold goes nowhere.

For Canadian buyers and sellers, the currency is half the story. Beyond the exchange rate, gold tracks real interest rates, central bank buying and selling, safe-haven demand during market stress, and jewellery and investment flows. Bank of Canada rate decisions and commodity-driven swings in the loonie often matter as much as headlines out of New York or London. A troy ounce is the standard quotation unit for precious metals worldwide, 31.1034768 grams. The same ounce breaks down to C$189.67 per gram and C$189,670.82 per kilogram today. Watch the USD/CAD rate alongside the spot price; in Canada, that pair decides how much of any gold move you actually feel.

03 Oct 2026

Info Edge Ventures, the venture capital arm of Info Edge (which owns Naukri), has co-led a $15 million funding round in Gravity, a Bengaluru-based startup that supplies interior materials. The round was announced on October 3, 2026, and was co-led by 3one4 Capital, with participation from Alteria Capital, Genesia Ventures, and a group of angel investors. The funding is a mix of equity and debt, though Gravity did not disclose the split. This investment is the main development for Info Edge today, as it deploys capital from its venture fund into a company operating in the home interiors space.

Gravity was founded by Saurabh Jain and Lalit Mittal, both former executives at Livspace, a home interiors and renovation platform. Jain served as CEO for India at Livspace, where he helped scale the India business to an annualised revenue run rate of around Rs 1,500 crore. Mittal was Livspace's Chief Business Officer for India and led its expansion across 30 cities. Earlier, Jain co-founded DezignUp, a design community and marketplace that Livspace acquired in March 2015. Gravity is building a platform for India's interior materials sector, initially focusing on kitchens and wardrobes. It brings specialist businesses onto shared technology, distribution, procurement, key-account management, and operating infrastructure. Its customers include designers, architects, modular showrooms, and design-and-build studios that select and procure materials for interior projects. The startup aims to simplify a fragmented supply chain of dealers, distributors, and suppliers, improving availability, fulfilment, and pricing visibility. Gravity said the businesses in its initial portfolio have a combined revenue base running into several hundred crore rupees and are EBITDA positive. The fresh capital will be used to strengthen technology, distribution, and key-account infrastructure, expand into new categories, build the brand, and support working capital. Gravity plans to expand into doors and windows, lighting, wall surfaces, and home automation, using common infrastructure across categories rather than separate systems.

For Info Edge, this investment is a financial one, not a strategic acquisition. Info Edge is best known for its recruitment platform Naukri, but it also operates real estate portal 99acres and matrimony site Jeevansathi. Its venture arm, Info Edge Ventures, invests in early- to growth-stage startups. The $15 million round is a modest deployment relative to Info Edge's overall balance sheet, but it signals continued interest in the home interiors and materials space. The fact that Gravity's portfolio businesses are already EBITDA positive (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation, a measure of operating profitability) is notable. It suggests the startup is not just a pre-revenue bet but has cash-generating operations. However, the exact revenue figure is not disclosed, only described as "several hundred crore rupees." The equity-debt split is also undisclosed, which matters because debt carries repayment obligations and could affect Gravity's financial flexibility. The round was co-led by 3one4 Capital, an early-stage venture firm, and Info Edge Ventures, with Alteria Capital, a venture debt provider, also participating. This mix of equity and debt investors indicates that Gravity is funding both growth and working capital needs.

The investment is unlikely to have a material impact on Info Edge's near-term financials. Info Edge's stock price is driven primarily by its core recruitment business, which is sensitive to IT hiring trends, and by its other operating segments. Venture investments are typically marked to market and can contribute to other income or fair value changes, but a $15 million round is small compared to Info Edge's market capitalisation. The strategic rationale for Info Edge Ventures may be to gain exposure to a sector that complements its existing real estate and interiors interests, but there is no indication of a deeper integration with 99acres or other Info Edge properties. The main unknown is how Gravity will use the capital to scale and whether it can achieve profitability across new categories. The company's expansion into doors, windows, lighting, and home automation will require significant investment and could face competition from established players. The supply chain for interior materials is complex and fragmented, and Gravity's platform model will need to prove it can deliver consistent value to both suppliers and customers. The fact that the founding team has experience scaling Livspace is a positive, but Livspace itself has faced challenges in the past. For Info Edge shareholders, this news is a reminder that the company's venture arm remains active, but it is not a game-changer for the core business. The investment is small and the outcome uncertain. What to watch next: any further disclosures from Gravity about its revenue, growth, or profitability; additional funding rounds; and whether Info Edge Ventures increases its stake or involvement. Also, watch for any impact on Info Edge's other investments or its overall venture portfolio performance, which is reported in its quarterly results.

OpenAI/Anthropic/Kimi/Deepseek/OpenRouter

The eternal complement

04 Oct 2026

The essay argues that human progress has always depended less on raw genius than on the machinery and bureaucracy needed to turn ideas into reality, and that AI is about to change which of those two things is scarce. The authors point to the James Webb Space Telescope: Galileo widened our view of the universe with two lenses and a tube, while Webb required eighteen mirror segments engineered to fifty nanometer precision, three hundred organizations across fourteen countries, and ten billion dollars. That gap is the point. Research productivity has been falling even as spending rises. Sustaining Moore's law now takes more than eighteen times as many researchers as it did in the early 1970s, and economy-wide research productivity fell by a factor of forty-one while research effort rose twenty-three-fold. The technician workforce is growing twice as fast as the scientists.

The authors call the unglamorous capacity to execute "institutional intelligence," and treat it as a complement to frontier intelligence: more of one raises the value of the other. Their bet is that AI will spend most of its time on the execution side, writing code, searching literature, turning sketches into prototypes. That would let one person pursue projects that once needed a whole organization, and push creative work outside the institutions that currently gatekeep it. The thing to watch is whether that produces a wave of independent work or just a new set of bottlenecks. My view: the essay is right that execution, not ideas, is the binding constraint, and that is exactly why the incumbents with the biggest bureaucracies stand to gain most.
